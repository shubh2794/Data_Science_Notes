Data Quality & Governance

Garbage in, garbage out. Before modeling, data must be trustworthy — accurate, complete, deduplicated, and well-governed (lineage, access, privacy). At LLM scale, filtering and dedup quality often matter more than raw quantity.

Data Engineering Quality Governance

01 Why it dominates outcomes

  • Model quality is capped by data quality — errors, duplicates, and bias propagate straight into predictions.
  • For LLMs, careful filtering and deduplication of pretraining data is a bigger lever than adding more raw tokens.
  • Governance — who can use what data, how it's tracked — is increasingly a legal and ethical requirement, not optional.

03 Cleaning & filtering

  • Deduplication: exact and near-duplicate removal — critical for fair eval and to avoid memorization.
  • Outlier handling: detect and cap/flag/remove anomalies.
  • Validation rules & schemas: enforce types, ranges, and constraints at ingestion.
  • Quality filtering (LLMs): drop low-quality, toxic, or boilerplate text; keep diverse, high-signal data.

04 Label quality

  • Labels are data too — noisy labels cap model accuracy.
  • Inter-annotator agreement (e.g. Cohen's κ) measures how consistently humans label; low agreement signals an ambiguous task or unclear guidelines.
  • Adjudication, clear rubrics, and gold sets improve label reliability.

05 Governance & privacy

  • Lineage: track where data came from and how it was transformed.
  • Access control & compliance: who can use what, under which regulations (GDPR, etc.).
  • PII handling: detect (NER) and redact personal data; for formal privacy guarantees see Differential Privacy.

06 Takeaways

  • Quality caps the model — accuracy, completeness, consistency, dedup.
  • Filter & dedup aggressively, especially for LLM pretraining.
  • Label quality matters; measure it with inter-annotator agreement.
  • Govern lineage, access, and PII; formalize privacy with Differential Privacy.
