Attention

Part 4 ended on a counting argument. A recurrent encoder compresses a length-L source into one dₕ-dimensional vector, so the average fraction of the input that any decoder can recover is dₕ/(L·dₓ) — a rank, not a training outcome, and therefore not fixable by a better optimiser, a better initialisation or a bigger corpus. Part 4 measured that fraction to six decimal places and stopped. This page starts by removing the cause. Do not summarise the source at all: keep every encoder state, and give the decoder a learned, differentiable rule for deciding which of them to read at each step. The length dependence disappears in one line, and what replaces it is a mechanism with its own arithmetic, its own failure modes and its own hardware economics — which is what the remaining thirty-five sections are about.

The centre of this page is the mechanism, taken apart: the additive alignment score derived from the problem it solves, the dot-product replacement and why it won, the 1/√dₖ scaling derived from the variance of a dot product rather than asserted, the query/key/value abstraction as a differentiable dictionary lookup, and the framing everything else follows from — attention computes its own mixing matrix from the input, where a dense layer's is fixed and a convolution's is fixed and tied. From there: multi-head attention and what a head actually specialises in, masking and the −∞-before-softmax detail that fails quietly, the exact cost in multiply–accumulates and the sequence length at which the quadratic term genuinely starts to matter, what attention cannot do, and the KV cache — the object that decides how much a long-context model costs to run, and the thing MQA, GQA and MLA each attack in a different way.

Every number printed here was recomputed against an independent implementation before it was printed: the variance of the dot product, the backward pass (audited against central differences, worst relative error 1.3e−09), the mask arithmetic in three precisions, the MAC crossovers, the KV-cache sizes, the arithmetic intensities, the rank-collapse curves and the long-context margins. Four widely repeated claims did not survive the recomputation — the standard account of what the missing √dₖ does to the gradient (§06), the standard summary of what stops rank collapse (§22), the headline KV-cache reduction of the low-rank cache design (§28), and “attention dominates the cost” (§19). Each is taught as a discrepancy in place, with the measurement, rather than repeated.

01 Where part 4 stopped, and the one-line repair

Part 4's argument was a counting argument, so the repair has to be a counting repair. It is: stop throwing the states away. Everything else on this page is a consequence of taking that seriously.

Restate the bound exactly as part 4 left it. A linear recurrent encoder makes the context vector an explicit linear function of the whole input,

part 4's bound, quoted, not re-derivedC = h_L = ∑ₖ xₖ · ( U W⁽ᴸ⁻¹⁻ᵏ⁾ ) so C is a linear map from L·dₓ └────┬────┘ numbers into dₕ numbers, and Mₖ rank(map) ≤ min(L·dₓ, dₕ) mean recoverable fraction of the input = min(1, dₕ / (L·dₓ)) ↑ the length is in the DENOMINATOR

Look at where the length sits. L appears once, dividing, because the numerator dₕ is a constant chosen by the architect and the denominator L·dₓ is chosen by the data. Any repair must either put an L in the numerator or take it out of the denominator, and there are exactly two ways to put one in the numerator: grow dₕ with L, which means the model's width depends on its longest input and is therefore not a model; or keep L vectors instead of one. The second is free — the encoder already computed h₁ … h_L and then discarded all but the last.

the repair, and what it does to the boundBEFORE the decoder sees C = h_L dₕ numbers AFTER the decoder sees H = [h₁; h₂; …; h_L] L·dₕ numbers mean recoverable fraction = min(1, L·dₕ / (L·dₓ)) = min(1, dₕ/dₓ) ↑ ↑ the L's CANCEL and dₕ/dₓ does not depend on the source length AT ALL. For any encoder wide enough that dₕ ≥ dₓ, the bound is exactly 1 — every position of the input remains linearly recoverable, at every length.

That is the whole of the motivation, and it is worth being precise about what it does and does not say. It says the information is still there. It does not say the decoder can use it, because H is a variable-length object and a decoder step needs a fixed-size input. The mechanism that turns a variable-length set of vectors into one fixed-size vector, without reintroducing a length-dependent bottleneck, is attention: at each decode step, produce a different convex combination of the L states, with the coefficients computed from the state doing the reading. One fixed-size read per step, but a different read each time, and the choice of read is learned.

02 Alignment: the original formulation, derived from what it has to do

The decoder needs one fixed-size vector per step, and it has L of them to choose from. Write down the three requirements that choice must satisfy and the 2015 formulation is essentially forced.

At decode step i the decoder has a state sᵢ₋₁ — everything it has produced so far — and it has the encoder states h₁ … h_L. It must produce a single vector cᵢ to condition the next output on. Three requirements:

requirement 1
Fixed size, any L
cᵢ must have a shape the decoder can consume, and that shape cannot depend on L — otherwise the decoder's weights depend on the input length and it is not a model.
requirement 2
Different at every step
If cᵢ were the same for all i we would be back at part 4's single summary and the bound would return. The read must depend on sᵢ₋₁.
requirement 3
Differentiable end to end
There is no supervision for which source word to look at. The choice must be learned from the translation loss alone, which means the gradient has to flow through the choosing.

From the requirements to the three steps

Requirement 1 forces a reduction over the L states, and the only reduction that is linear, order-preserving and shape-preserving is a weighted sum. Requirement 2 forces the weights to be a function of sᵢ₋₁. Requirement 3 forces the function to be smooth. Put together:

the mechanism, in the three steps it has always had score eᵢⱼ = a( sᵢ₋₁ , hⱼ ) for every j = 1 … L normalise αᵢⱼ = exp(eᵢⱼ) / ∑ₖ exp(eᵢₖ) so αᵢⱼ ≥ 0, ∑ⱼ αᵢⱼ = 1 blend cᵢ = ∑ⱼ αᵢⱼ · hⱼ ONE vector, dₕ wide, any L and the alignment function a( · , · ) that the 2015 model useda(s, h) = vᵀ · tanh( s·W_a + h·U_a ) ← ADDITIVE / concat scoring shapes, in the row convention of §01: s (1 × d_s) W_a (d_s × d_a) h (1 × dₕ) U_a (dₕ × d_a) v (d_a) a single vector the score is a SCALAR: a one-hidden-layer network with d_a units, one output, no output bias, applied to the pair (s, h).

Three design decisions hiding in the formula

Three things about that formula are worth naming, because each is a design decision and not a detail.

  • It is a small MLP, and it is the smallest one that can work. A linear score sW + hU would be additively separable: the hU term is the same for every query, so it would shift all scores by a constant and, after the softmax, do nothing except add a query-independent prior over source positions. The tanh is what makes the score depend on the interaction between the query and the key rather than on each separately. §04's dot product gets the interaction a different way.
  • The two projections have different input widths on purpose. d_s and dₕ need not match: additive attention can score a query against a key of a completely different dimension, which is why it survives in settings where the two sides are genuinely different objects. The dot product cannot; it requires dₖ on both sides, which is exactly why W_Q and W_K exist at all (§07).
  • The softmax is what makes the choice a choice. It is not there to be probabilistic. It is there because it maps L unconstrained real numbers onto the simplex — non-negative and summing to one — so that cᵢ is a convex combination of the states and therefore lives in their convex hull, at the same scale as any single state, for any L. Replace the softmax with, say, raw scores and cᵢ's norm grows with L; replace it with a sigmoid applied per position and the sum grows with L too. §06 shows what else the softmax quietly guarantees, and §23 shows what it costs.

The alignment matrix

The bottom strip of that figure is the object the mechanism is usually judged by: the alignment matrix α, one row per decode step and one column per source position. Nothing in the loss asks for it to be interpretable. It becomes roughly monotone on language pairs with similar word order because that is what minimises the translation loss, and it stops being monotone exactly where the languages disagree about order — which is the first piece of evidence anyone had that the mechanism was learning something structural rather than memorising. §33 revisits how much weight that evidence can bear.

Where this landed historically. The formulation above was introduced to fix translation quality on long sentences, and the effect it fixed is measurable: without it, quality falls off sharply once the source is longer than the sentences the model was trained on, because the fixed summary is being asked to hold more than §01's bound permits; with it, the fall-off largely disappears. Machine Translation owns that story and the evaluation metrics that measured it.

03 What "soft" alignment means, and why the hard version is a different problem

"Soft" is not a hedge or an approximation of something better. It is the choice that makes the selection trainable by gradient descent at all, and the alternative is not a slightly worse attention — it is a reinforcement-learning problem.

The honest way to see this is to write down the hard version and try to differentiate it. Hard selection picks one source position:

hard selection, and the wall it hitsSOFT cᵢ = ∑ⱼ αᵢⱼ hⱼ α = softmax(e) ∂cᵢ/∂e exists everywhere HARD cᵢ = h_{j*} where j* = argmaxⱼ eᵢⱼ ∂ j* / ∂ e = 0 almost everywhere, and UNDEFINED on the ties. The argmax is piecewise constant. Its gradient carries no information about how to change the scores, because a small change in e leaves j* alone — until it does not, and then it jumps. There is nothing for the chain rule to propagate. the two standard escapes, and what each costs1 · SAMPLE and use a score-function estimator (REINFORCE): j ∼ α ∇ E[loss] = E[ loss · ∇ log α_j ] unbiased, and its VARIANCE grows with L and with the loss scale. It needs a baseline, many samples, or both. 2 · RELAX: keep the sample but reparametrise it (Gumbel-softmax / concrete), giving a biased but low-variance gradient, with a temperature that has to be annealed. 3 · DO NOT CHOOSE. Take the convex combination and let the WEIGHTS carry the choice. Gradient is exact, variance is zero, no extra hyper-parameter. This is soft attention, and it is the reason the mechanism was usable immediately.

The figure measures the difference rather than asserting it: the same tiny selection problem, solved by the exact soft gradient and by the sampled hard gradient, with the spread of each plotted against the number of candidates — the whole gradient vector's, relative to the exact gradient, which is the quantity that grows like √L.

Soft against hard: the same objective, the exact gradient against the sampled one the right panel is the one to watch: the sampled estimator's spread relative to the exact gradient grows like √L when the scores are of order one, and the soft gradient has none, because there is no sampling in it
The trade being made, stated plainly. Soft attention reads everything and weights it. That is more expensive than reading one position — O(L) work instead of O(1) — and it is the origin of the quadratic cost in §18. Hard attention would be cheaper at inference and is genuinely used where the candidate set is enormous (retrieval over millions of documents, where reading everything is not an option). The reason soft won inside the model is not that it is cheaper; it is that it turns a discrete decision into a smooth one, and gradient descent can only optimise the second kind.
The common phrasing. "Soft attention is a differentiable approximation to hard attention." Backwards. Hard attention is the special case that soft attention reaches in the limit of infinitely sharp scores, and it is the one that loses the property being relied on. Calling the smooth thing an approximation of the non-smooth thing puts the causality the wrong way round and makes the softmax look like a compromise instead of the load-bearing part.

The usual summary. "Additive and multiplicative attention perform similarly; multiplicative is faster, so we use it." 05 Why the 1/√dₖ is there, derived rather than asserted

The scale is not a tuning constant and it is not "for numerical stability". It is the exact standardisation of a sum of dₖ independent products, and the derivation takes three lines.

Take the assumption the scale is derived under and state it plainly: the components of q and k are independent, mean 0, variance 1. That is not true of a trained model, but it is close to true at initialisation, which is when the decision has to be made — a layer that saturates at step zero never gets to step one.

the variance of a dot product, exactlyq·kᵀ = ∑_{c=1..dₖ} q_c k_c with q_c, k_c independent, E = 0, Var = 1 E[ q_c k_c ] = E[q_c]·E[k_c] = 0 independence Var[ q_c k_c ] = E[q_c²k_c²] − 0 = E[q_c²]·E[k_c²] = 1 independence again The dₖ terms are independent, so the variances ADD: E[ q·kᵀ ] = 0 Var[ q·kᵀ ] = dₖ sd[ q·kᵀ ] = √dₖ so the scale is a z-score and nothing more q·kᵀ / √dₖ has mean 0 and variance 1, for EVERY dₖ. That is the whole derivation. The factor is √dₖ because a sum of dₖ independent unit-variance terms has standard deviation √dₖ, the same reason a random walk of dₖ steps travels √dₖ. Not dₖ, not √d_model, not √(dₖ·h) — √dₖ, the width of ONE head's key.

What the derivation assumes, and what it is worth when the assumption fails. It needs only that the components be uncorrelated with equal second moments — not Gaussian, not identically distributed, not even continuous. The figure checks that by swapping in a uniform, a two-point and a heavy-tailed distribution and getting the same law. What it genuinely needs is the independence across components, and that is what a trained model breaks: after training, q and k live in a low-dimensional, strongly correlated subspace, and the effective number of independent directions is far below dₖ. The scale is therefore right at initialisation and approximate afterwards — which is exactly why §06's failure mode is a training-time phenomenon rather than an initialisation one, and why some models add an explicit normalisation of q and k on top of the fixed scale.

The standard sentence is "for large dₖ the dot products grow, pushing the softmax into regions where it has extremely small gradients". Now the gradient claim, checked

The softmax Jacobian is the object the claim is about:

the softmax Jacobian, and why it dies at BOTH ends ∂αᵢ/∂sⱼ = αᵢ(δᵢⱼ − αⱼ) J = diag(α) − ααᵀ α uniform (αᵢ = 1/n): ‖J‖_F = √(1/n − 1/n²) → 0 as n grows α one-hot: J = 0 EXACTLY in between: ‖J‖_F is largest at MODERATE concentration — for α = (½, ½, 0, …) it is exactly ½. So ‖J‖_F is not monotone in the sharpness, and therefore not monotone in dₖ.

That non-monotonicity is visible in the table and it is where the standard sentence over-reaches. The mean ‖J‖_F rises from 0.184 at dₖ = 1 to 0.295 at dₖ = 16, and at dₖ = 64 it is still 0.210 — larger than the scaled value of 0.184. On the mean, the unscaled head at the original design point has a bigger softmax gradient, not a smaller one.

The claim is nonetheless true, and the table shows in which sense. Look at the median and the 5th percentile. At dₖ = 1024 the median ‖J‖_F is 3e−04 against the scaled 0.180 — a factor of 600 — and the 5th percentile is 1.3e−15, which is not small but zero to machine precision. The precise version of the claim. 07 Query, key, value: a dictionary lookup you can differentiate

The three names are not decoration. They come from a data structure, and the analogy is exact enough to be worth spelling out — including the two places it breaks, which is where the interesting design freedom lives.

a hash table, written as arithmetic table { k₁: v₁ , k₂: v₂ , … , k_n: v_n } lookup table[q] = vⱼ where kⱼ = q exact match, or an error as arithmetic: out = ∑ⱼ 1[ kⱼ = q ] · vⱼ └────┬────┘ an indicator: 1 on exactly one j, 0 elsewhere soften the indicator and you have attention out = ∑ⱼ α(q, kⱼ) · vⱼ with αⱼ ≥ 0, ∑ⱼ αⱼ = 1 α = softmax over j of a similarity score. THREE changes, each deliberate: 1 match becomes DEGREE OF MATCH an indicator becomes a weight 2 one result becomes a BLEND the output need not be any stored vᵢ 3 equality becomes SIMILARITY IN A LEARNED SPACE which is what W_Q and W_K are for

Change 3 is the one that carries the load, and it is why the projections exist rather than the raw vectors being used directly.

what the projections buy, in the row convention of §01 Q = X·W_Q (n × dₖ) "what this position is looking for" K = X·W_K (n × dₖ) "what this position offers to be found by" V = X·W_V (n × d_v) "what this position hands over if found" Without them the score would be xᵢ·xⱼᵀ, which is SYMMETRIC and fixed: position i finds j exactly as strongly as j finds i, and the notion of similarity is whatever the embedding happened to produce. With them: score(i,j) = xᵢ W_Q W_Kᵀ xⱼᵀ = xᵢ · M · xⱼᵀ with M = W_Q W_Kᵀ a general BILINEAR form, learned, and NOT symmetric unless M happens to be. "the adjective looks for the noun it modifies" and "the noun looks for its adjective" are different relations, and one matrix expresses both. and the separation of K from V is the other half K decides WHO gets read. V decides WHAT is read. Tying them (K = V) would force the feature that makes a position findable to also be the feature it contributes — so a token could not be easy to find for one reason and useful for a different one. Keeping them separate costs one extra matrix and is why attention can implement "look up the token after the one that matches" (§33) at all.

Two places the dictionary analogy genuinely breaks, both of which matter later:

  • There is no "key not found". The weights are a softmax, so they sum to one whatever the scores are. A query that matches nothing does not return nothing — it returns the least-bad blend of everything. §32 shows what that does at long context, and it is the mechanism behind attention sinks: models learn to keep a semantically empty position around precisely so that a query with nothing to say has somewhere to put its unavoidable unit of weight.
  • The rank of M = W_Q W_Kᵀ is at most dₖ. With dₖ < d_model — which is always, in multi-head attention — the bilinear form is low-rank by construction. A head cannot express an arbitrary relation between positions; it can express one whose matrix has rank at most dₖ. §13 measures what that costs and why it is nevertheless the right trade.
From an exact lookup to attention, one relaxation at a time the temperature slider walks between the two: at the left end the output is one stored value exactly, at the right end it is the plain mean of all of them
The one-sentence version to keep. Attention is a dictionary lookup in which the match is a learned, asymmetric similarity rather than equality, the result is a blend rather than a hit, and every part of it is differentiable — so the model can learn what to store under which key and what to ask for from the downstream loss alone, with no supervision about the lookup itself.

10 The framing everything else follows from: a weight matrix computed from the input

This is the deepest thing on the page, and it is one equation. Write the output of a single head as a matrix acting on the values and the whole series lines up behind it.

one head, with the mixing made explicit Z = A · V A = softmax( X W_Q W_Kᵀ Xᵀ / √dₖ ) ─ ─ ─ an (n × n) matrix that MIXES ACROSS POSITIONS, and it is a function of X. Compare, for the same job — producing each output position as a combination of input positions — with the two operators the earlier parts built: dense layer over positions Z = A_dense · V A_dense is a LEARNED PARAMETER: n² free numbers, fixed after training, and its size fixes the sequence length convolution Z = A_conv · V A_conv is BANDED and TIED: f free numbers repeated down the diagonals (part 3's Toeplitz matrix), fixed after training attention Z = A(X) · V A is COMPUTED. It has ZERO free parameters of its own; the parameters are in the FUNCTION that produces it, and the matrix itself is different for every input.

Everything the architecture is good and bad at is in that comparison.

  • The parameter count stops depending on the sequence length. A dense mixing matrix over n positions needs n² parameters and can only ever handle that n. Attention's mixing has none — it is produced by W_Q and W_K, which are (d × dₖ) and know nothing about n. That is why the same weights run at 512 tokens and at 128 000.
  • The cost starts depending on the sequence length. The matrix is not stored, so it must be built — n² scores, every forward pass. §18 counts it exactly. This is the price of the previous point, and it is the same trade in both directions.
  • The prior is weak, and it is weak on purpose. A convolution asserts that the useful relations are local and position-independent. Attention asserts almost nothing about which positions should be related, only that whatever the relation is, it should be computable as a low-rank bilinear form on the representations. Part 1 §24's table is organised around exactly this axis, and its transformer row says it in one line: the mixing matrix is computed from the data rather than learned as a fixed parameter. This section is that row, derived.
  • It is a second-order operation. A depends on X and then multiplies V, which also depends on X. The output is therefore quadratic in the input even before any activation function — Z ≈ (XW_QW_KᵀXᵀ)XW_V to first order in the softmax. A dense layer and a convolution are both linear in their input. That multiplicative interaction is a genuine increase in expressive power per layer, and it is why attention can implement operations like "find the token that matches this one and copy what follows it" (§33) that no fixed linear mixing can.
The same picture for three operators: change the input and see which matrices move the change input button is the experiment — press it and only one of the three mixing matrices is different afterwards
The name for this, and where else it appears. An operation whose weights are produced by a network from its own input is dynamic or input-conditioned; hypernetworks, squeeze-and-excitation gates in convolutional networks, the gates of an LSTM and the router of a mixture of experts are all instances. Attention is the instance where the produced object is an n × n matrix over positions, which is why it is the one that changed how sequences are modelled. Recognising the pattern makes the LSTM's forget gate and attention's weight matrix the same idea at two scales: the network decides, per input, how much of something to let through.

11 Attention does not know about order — proved, measured, and handed over

A convolution knows what "next to" means because its matrix is banded. Attention's matrix is built from content alone, so it has no notion of position whatsoever. That is a two-line proof, it is exactly measurable, and it is the reason part 6 has to do something about it.

12 Multi-head attention: why one head is not enough, stated as a limitation

The usual justification — "different heads capture different relationships" — is true and unfalsifiable as stated. There is a sharper version that is a capacity argument, and it predicts exactly when adding a head helps and when it does not.

what one head can and cannot produce zᵢ = ∑ⱼ αᵢⱼ vⱼ αᵢⱼ ≥ 0, ∑ⱼ αᵢⱼ = 1 For output position i, ONE head produces exactly ONE convex combination of the value rows. That is one point in the convex hull of {v₁ … v_n}. So if position i needs to report TWO unrelated facts about two DIFFERENT positions — "the token before me" AND "the first token of the sentence" — one head must place its single weight vector somewhere between them, and the two facts arrive AVERAGED, in the same dₖ-dimensional slot, with no way for a downstream position-wise layer to separate them again. h heads produce h SEPARATE convex combinations, written into h DISJOINT blocks of the concatenated output. Two facts, two slots, no interference.

The reason "just make the single head wider" does not fix it is worth stating explicitly: widening a head widens d_v, giving each value more room, but it does not add a second set of weights. The bottleneck is the single row αᵢ, not the width of what it multiplies. It is a selection bottleneck, not a capacity bottleneck, and only more heads relieve it.

At every position t of a random symbol sequence, the model must emit three things at once: the token at t−1, the token at position 0, and the token at t itself. The claim to be careful with. "Head k is the syntax head." Heads are only defined up to permutation, and the assignment is a property of the run, not of the architecture. Statements of the form "some head implements X, reproducibly across seeds" are well supported; statements about a numbered head in a particular checkpoint are about that checkpoint.

13 The head-dimension trade: h·dₖ = d, and what it costs each head

Splitting a width-d block into h heads of width d/h keeps the parameter count and the arithmetic almost exactly constant. It is not free — each head gets a strictly weaker relation to express — and the constraint is a rank.

the constraint, and where it bindsStandard choice: dₖ = d_v = d / h so h·dₖ = d parameters d·dₖ·(h+g) + d·d_v·(g+h) = 4d² when g = h — INDEPENDENT of h quadratic MACs 2 n² · h·dₖ = 2 n² d — INDEPENDENT of h So h is FREE in both budgets. So the trade is: h relations of rank d/h each, against one relation of rank d. The total rank across heads, h · (d/h) = d, is the same either way — which is why the arithmetic does not change — but the partition is different, and it is the partition that matters, because §12 showed the binding constraint is the number of separate weight rows, not the total rank.

14 Concat-and-project: what W_O is actually doing

The last step of a multi-head block looks like plumbing — glue the heads together and multiply. Cut W_O into blocks and it stops being plumbing: the block output is a sum of per-head contributions, each with its own private output matrix, and every statement about "what a head writes into the residual stream" follows from that one rewriting.

15 Masking: the two kinds, and what each is actually protecting

A mask forbids some query–key pairs. There are two of them in practice, they protect against completely different failures, and they are almost always applied together and combined by addition.

causal / look-ahead
Protects the training objective
Position i may read only j ≤ i. Without it, a language model trained to predict token i+1 can simply read token i+1 — the loss goes to zero and the model has learned nothing. It is not a modelling choice; it is what makes the objective non-trivial.
padding
Protects the batch
Sequences in a batch have different lengths, so short ones are padded to the longest. Padding tokens are an artefact of the batching, not data. Without a mask they contribute to every weighted sum, and a model's output for a sentence would depend on which other sentences were in its batch.
window / sparsity
Protects the budget
A structural mask that forbids far-away pairs to save the n² cost (§29). Mechanically identical to the other two; motivated by arithmetic rather than by correctness.

The three ways to get it wrong

error 1
Forgetting the row-sum term
Writing dS = A ⊙ dA. This is the gradient of a normalisation-free exponential, not of a softmax. It is not orthogonal to the ones vector, so it pushes all scores up or down together — a direction the softmax is blind to — and training becomes mysteriously slow rather than broken.
error 2
Applying the scale once
The 1/√dₖ multiplies the score, so it must appear in ∂L/∂S exactly once, before both ∂L/∂Q and ∂L/∂K. Applying it to only one of them gives a gradient that is correct in direction for Q and wrong by √dₖ for K — an 11× error at dₖ = 128, which Adam will partially hide.
error 3
Reusing the mask
Adding the mask again in the backward pass, or re-masking ∂L/∂S. Unnecessary: A is already exactly zero there, so ∂L/∂S is too. Harmless with −∞; with a finite constant it silently multiplies the leaked gradient by a second copy of the mask.

21 What attention cannot do (1): it mixes tokens and does nothing to features

Look at where the non-linearity in an attention block actually is. The softmax is non-linear across positions. Along the feature axis, the entire path from input to output is a chain of matrix multiplications — and a chain of matrix multiplications is one matrix.

This is why the position-wise feedforward sub-layer is not an accessory. Part 1's whole argument — that depth plus a point-wise non-linearity is what buys the function class — applies to the feature axis, and attention supplies none of it. That is the structure interpretability work exploits (§33), the structure the rank-collapse theorem of §22 is proved in, and the reason "attention is a communication primitive and the MLP is a computation primitive" is a genuinely accurate slogan rather than a nice-sounding one.

The usual summary. "Skip connections and MLPs stop the degeneration." Measured with random weights, the MLP on its own makes it worse, and it is the normalisation that does the work. Compare the columns at layer 12: skip alone is at 0.302, skip plus MLP is at 0.120 — the MLP accelerated the collapse — and skip plus MLP plus pre-LayerNorm is at 0.588 and has essentially stopped falling. The reason is scale: a residual branch X + f(X) with an untrained ReLU MLP amplifies whatever component is largest, which is the shared mean, whereas pre-normalisation re-standardises each token before every branch and denies the mean that advantage. The theoretical result is a statement about upper bounds under assumptions about the branch's Lipschitz constant; the measurement is about a specific untrained network. Both are right, and the practical lesson is the second: the pre-norm is load-bearing here, not decorative.

Why the quality cost is smaller than the compression suggests

  • The query side is untouched. §12 established that the binding constraint is the number of independent selections, and that is h, not g.   • Heads within a group were already correlated. §12's own measurement showed seeds putting two heads on the same relation. Real models have measurable redundancy between neighbouring heads, and a shared key/value set is a way of paying for it once.
  • It can be added to an existing model cheaply. Attention sinks: why the first token has to stay

This is the most useful non-obvious fact about windowed attention, and it follows directly from §07's observation that a softmax has no "no match" output.

The argument. A softmax row sums to one whatever the scores are. A query with nothing relevant to attend to must still put its full unit of weight somewhere. Models solve this by learning a sink: a position that is semantically empty and that everyone can reach, which absorbs the leftover mass. In a causal model the natural candidate is the first token, because it is visible to every subsequent position. Evict it — which a naive rolling window does the moment the sequence exceeds w — and every row's spare mass has nowhere to go, so it is redistributed onto genuinely irrelevant recent tokens, and quality falls off a cliff at exactly the point where the window first slides past position 0. Keeping four tokens from the very beginning, permanently, restores it. That is a change of four entries in a mask, and it is the difference between a model that streams indefinitely and one that degrades the instant its buffer wraps.

The one piece of mathematics it needs: an online softmax

You cannot normalise a softmax until you have seen every score, and the whole point is not to hold every score. The resolution is a running rescaling, and it is exact:

combining a running result with one new block, exactly state after some blocks: m (running max), ℓ (running sum), O (running output) new block gives: m̃ (its max), ℓ̃ (its sum), Õ (its output) m′ = max(m, m̃) ℓ′ = e⁽ᵐ⁻ᵐ′⁾·ℓ + e⁽ᵐ̃⁻ᵐ′⁾·ℓ̃ O′ = ( e⁽ᵐ⁻ᵐ′⁾·ℓ·O + e⁽ᵐ̃⁻ᵐ′⁾·ℓ̃·Õ ) / ℓ′ Every term is bounded by 1 because both exponents are ≤ 0, so this is as overflow-safe as the ordinary max-subtracting softmax — it IS the ordinary max-subtracting softmax, applied incrementally. The result is EXACT: not an approximation, not a low-rank sketch, not a sparsity pattern. The same numbers, in a different order.
Why the backward pass is the interesting half. The backward pass needs A (§17), and A was never stored. The fix is to recompute it, block by block, from Q, K and the saved row statistics (m, ℓ) — which costs extra arithmetic and saves the same quadratic memory traffic. It is gradient checkpointing applied to one operation, and it wins for exactly the reason §26 gives: the arithmetic was never the binding resource. This is also why the reported end-to-end speedups vary so much by sequence length — 15% on a 512-token model, 3× at 1K, more at longer — since the fraction of time spent in attention is itself a function of §19's crossover.

Tiled attention with an online softmax: the running state, and the traffic it avoids step through the key blocks and watch the running max, sum and output update — the final result is checked against the direct computation
Two things it is not. It is not an approximation — the output is the same function, and any difference from a one-shot computation is floating-point reassociation at the level of round-off. And it does not reduce the O(n²) arithmetic — §18's MAC count is unchanged, to the operation. What it removes is the n² materialisation, which is why its headline effect is on the maximum sequence length that fits (§20) at least as much as on speed.

33 Attention as retrieval, and the circuit that made in-context learning legible

§07 framed attention as a differentiable lookup. The strongest evidence that this is what models actually do with it is a two-layer circuit that can be written down exactly, that appears in real trained models, and whose appearance during training coincides with a step change in their behaviour.

The pattern

what an induction head does given a context that contains … A B … … … A predict B "find the previous occurrence of the token I am now looking at, and predict whatever followed it." Note that this requires NO knowledge of what A or B mean, and no weights specific to them. It is a pure copy-with-lookup, and it generalises to tokens the model has never seen together — which is exactly what "learning from the context" means. the circuit, in two layers, and why it cannot be done in one LAYER 1 — a "previous-token" head position s attends to s−1 and COPIES that token's identity into a spare subspace of position s's representation. After layer 1, position s carries two things: "I am token x_s" and "I follow x_{s−1}". LAYER 2 — the induction head query at position t reads "I am token x_t" key at position s reads "I follow x_{s−1}" score(t, s) is large exactly when x_{s−1} = x_t value at position s is "I am token x_s" output = the token that followed the previous occurrence of x_t. WHY TWO LAYERS. Layer 2's key must describe the token BEFORE s, and a single attention layer's key is a function of position s alone. Some earlier layer has to move x_{s−1} into position s first. §14's additivity also matters here: the two heads compose ACROSS layers, through the representation, which is the only way heads can interact at all.

Two facts make this more than a cute construction. First, heads with exactly this behaviour are found in real trained transformers, and they can be identified by a behavioural test — does the head attend from a token to the position after that token's previous occurrence — rather than by inspecting weights. Second, the point in training at which they appear coincides with a visible bump in the loss curve and with a sharp increase in the model's ability to use its context, which is about as close to a mechanistic explanation of an emergent capability as this field has produced.

A hand-wired induction circuit, and what breaks when you remove half of it the ablation switch is the experiment: delete the previous-token head and the second layer's attention becomes meaningless
What interpretability has and has not established here. Established: that specific, describable algorithms are implemented by identifiable small groups of heads; that the algorithm above is one of them; that §14's factorisation into a "where it reads" matrix W_Q W_Kᵀ and a "what it writes" matrix W_V W_O is the right basis for describing them, because only those products are invariant to the arbitrary basis inside a head. Not established: that most of what a large model does decomposes this way, that a head's role is stable across seeds (§12 measured that it is not), or that reading an attention map is a reliable explanation of a prediction — a head can attend strongly to a token and write almost nothing about it, since where a head reads and what it writes are two different matrices. Model Interpretability owns the general treatment.

The row that changed the field is the third from the bottom. A recurrence connects two positions through a power of one matrix, and part 4 spent ten sections on the consequences: the gradient decays like ρᵈ and the entire LSTM exists to make ρ ≈ 1 by construction. Attention connects them through a single weight, so there is no product to decay and the distance does not appear in the gradient path at all. The same information-moving job, three ways the gradient-at-distance panel is measured by perturbation, not by evaluating a formula
The synthesis part 1 §24 was building towards. Every one of these is a constrained dense layer over positions. A convolution constrains the mixing matrix to be banded and tied. A recurrence constrains it to be generated by powers of a single matrix. Attention removes the constraint on which entries may be non-zero and replaces it with a constraint on how the entries are produced — from the data, by a low-rank bilinear form. The prior gets weaker in that order, the sample efficiency falls in that order, and the ceiling given enough data rises in that order. That is the same trade the whole domain is organised around, applied to one axis.

35 Pitfalls, in the order they are usually met

Each of these is something that runs, trains, and produces a wrong or worse model without raising an error. That is the selection criterion — things that crash are not on this list.

shapes
A strided head split
Taking every h-th column for head 1 instead of the first dₖ. Both partition the same columns, so nothing throws. Different model, incompatible with anyone else's checkpoint. §08.
conventions
Mixing row and column layouts
Half a derivation in xW and half in Wᵀx. Square matrices transpose into square matrices, so it runs. §01 declares one and never leaves it.
masking
Zeroing after the softmax
Rows stop summing to one, so the output is rescaled by however much mass the mask removed — and by more for later positions under a causal mask. A position-dependent shrinkage of the output. §15.
masking
A finite mask constant on an all-masked row
The softmax is shift-invariant, so a constant added to every entry does nothing. The layer attends at full strength to positions that were all supposed to be forbidden. −∞ gives NaN, which is the report you want. §16.
masking
A padding mask on the query axis
Padding needs silencing as a key, not as a query. Masking the query axis creates all-masked rows and therefore the previous bug. §15.
masking
−1e9 under mixed precision
Not representable in float16, so it silently becomes −∞ on the cast — and a latent all-masked-row bug that was invisible in float32 starts producing NaN, reported as "AMP is unstable". §16.
gradients
Dropping the row-sum term
dS = A ⊙ dA instead of A ⊙ (dA − ⟨dA, A⟩). Adds a component the softmax is blind to; training is slower and nothing indicates why. §17.
gradients
Materialising the softmax Jacobian
Forming the (n × n) Jacobian per row instead of using the two-term identity. Correct, and n times slower on the largest tensor in the model. §17.
scaling
Scaling by √d_model instead of √dₖ
A factor of √h — 5.7× at eight heads. The layer starts far more uniform than intended, and on tasks whose answer is a selection it never sharpens. §05.
variants
Expecting GQA to speed up prefill
GQA shrinks the cache and the projections; the quadratic term is untouched. It buys a factor of h/g at decode and close to nothing on a long prompt. §27.
variants
A rolling window that evicts token 0
The first token is the attention sink. Evicting it at the moment the buffer wraps removes the only place a query with no match can put its unavoidable unit of weight, and quality falls off a cliff at exactly that point. Keep a few. §29.
reasoning
"Attention dominates the cost"
At their trained context lengths the quadratic term is 10–24% of a layer's arithmetic for every configuration in §19's table. The binding costs are the n² activation memory at training time and the KV-cache bandwidth at inference. §19, §20, §26.
reasoning
Reading an attention map as an explanation
Where a head reads (W_Q W_Kᵀ) and what it writes (W_V W_O) are different matrices. A head can attend strongly to a token and contribute almost nothing about it. §14, §33.
reasoning
Naming a numbered head's job
Heads are defined up to permutation and their assignment is a property of the run. §12 measured three of six seeds giving a clean split and three not. §12.

36 What to remember, and exactly where part 6 begins

The mechanism, in the order it was built

  • Attention exists because a fixed-size summary has a rank. Part 4's bound was dₕ/(L·dₓ); keeping every encoder state makes it dₕ/dₓ and the length cancels (§01). Everything else is the cost of that cancellation.
  • Score, normalise, blend. Three steps, and the softmax is load-bearing: it puts the weights on the simplex, so the output is a convex combination whose norm cannot grow with n (§02), and it makes a discrete selection differentiable, which is why the gradient has zero variance instead of the sampled estimator's (§03).
  • 1/√dₖ is a z-score. Var[q·kᵀ] = dₖ exactly, for uncorrelated unit-variance components, whatever their distribution (§05). Without it an untrained head at dₖ = 64 already puts 79% of its weight on one arbitrary key, and on a task whose answer is a spread rather than a pick it never recovers (§06).
  • Q, K, V is a differentiable dictionary lookup in which equality has become a learned, asymmetric, rank-dₖ similarity and a hit has become a blend (§07). There is no "not found": the weights sum to one whatever the scores are, which is the origin of attention sinks (§29) and of the long-context dilution of §32.
  • The framing to carry away: attention computes its own mixing matrix from the input (§10). A dense layer's is a fixed parameter and needs n² of them; a convolution's is fixed, banded and tied; attention's has zero free parameters and is a function of X. That is why the same weights run at any length, why the cost is quadratic, and why the prior is weak.
  • Attention is permutation-equivariant, exactly (§11) — measured at 5.6e−16 — so position must be injected. A causal mask breaks the symmetry on its own, which is why the requirement is about quality rather than possibility.
  • Multi-head is a selection bottleneck, not a capacity one (§12): one head is one convex combination per position, so k simultaneous relations need at least k heads, and the loss falls four orders of magnitude between one head and three and not at all between three and four. The head count is free in both budgets; what it costs is the rank d/h of each head's relation (§13).
  • Y = ∑ⱼ Aⱼ X (W_Vʲ W_Oʲ) (§14). Heads are additive and do not interact within a block; each is two low-rank matrices, one for where it reads and one for what it writes. Every later section is stated in this form.
  • Masks are additive and −∞ (§15–§16). A finite constant is a no-op on an all-masked row, by shift invariance, and the NaN that −∞ produces is a feature.
  • The quadratic term is a minority of the arithmetic at every real configuration's trained context — 10–24% (§19) — and overtakes everything else only at n ≈ 2d + nmat·d_ff/2. What binds is memory: h·n² activations at training time (§20) and KV-cache bandwidth at inference (§26), where the arithmetic intensity is h/(g·b) and does not improve with batch.
  • MQA, GQA and MLA are three ways to shrink the same object. Share the key/value heads (h/g less cache, quadratic term untouched), or compress them into a latent ((d_c + dₕᴿ) per token, absorbed into W_Q and W_O so the expansion is free). §28 checks the headline number and finds it is architecture plus quantisation, not architecture alone.
  • FlashAttention changes the memory traffic and nothing else (§31): same result to round-off, same MAC count, Θ(n²d²/M) HBM accesses instead of Θ(nd + n²), via an online softmax that is exact.
  • What attention cannot do: it supplies no per-token non-linearity (§21 — with one token, any depth of attention is one matrix multiply); stacked alone it collapses every token to the same vector within two or three layers (§22); and its rows fail at both ends of the entropy scale (§23).
