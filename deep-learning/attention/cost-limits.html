18 The cost of one layer, counted term by term

One layer costs three kinds of multiply–accumulate: the four projections (4nd², linear in the sequence length), the two n × n products with the keys and values (2n²d, quadratic in it), and the feedforward sub-layer (nmat·n·d·d_ff, linear again). They scale differently, and getting them separate is the whole prerequisite for §19, which computes when the quadratic one actually dominates.

Only the lower triangle is needed, and a kernel that skips fully-masked BLOCKS actually achieves it: quad_causal = 2 n² d · (n+1)/(2n) ≈ n² d GROUPED-QUERY ATTENTION shrinks the PROJECTION term, not the quadratic one. K and V get g heads instead of h: proj_gqa = 2 n d² + 2 n d (g·dₖ) so at g = h/4: 2.5 n d² instead of 4 n d² The quadratic term is UNCHANGED by GQA, because the KV heads are shared, not fewer — every query head still scores against a full set of n keys. This is a common misreading of GQA.

And a term that does not appear in the MAC count at all but decides whether the layer runs: the softmax itself. It is h·n² exponentials, a divide per entry, and — crucially — h·n² elements that have to exist somewhere. §20 counts that; §31 is about a kernel that arranges for them not to.

19 When the quadratic term actually dominates — computed, not asserted

The quadratic term overtakes the two linear ones at a sequence length with a closed form, n* = 2d + nmat·d_ff/2 — of the order of 5 000 to 50 000 tokens for real models — and below that length the projections and the feedforward sub-layer, not attention's n² term, are most of the arithmetic. That is why "attention is quadratic, so it dominates the cost", the most repeated sentence about this architecture, is for most real configurations at their trained context length false. Here is the crossover, computed.

Read the last two columns. At the length these models were trained to run at, the quadratic term is 10–24% of a layer's arithmetic. For Llama-3 70B at its 8 192-token context it is 13.56%: seven-eighths of the arithmetic in a "quadratic-cost architecture" is the linear-in-n part. The one entry above 50% is Mistral-7B quoted at its 32 768-token context — and the first release of that model used a 4 096-token sliding window precisely so that it did not pay it (§29).

The honest summary, in three clauses. (1) The quadratic term is genuinely quadratic and eventually wins — at n = 131 072 it is 83% of a Llama-3 8B layer. (2) "Eventually" is n ≈ 2d + nmat·d_ff/2 (less at g < h), which is three to nine times the context they were trained at for every model in the table except Mistral-7B — whose 32 768-token context sits at 1.2× its own crossover, which is why its first release used a 4 096-token sliding window (§29). (3) The reason attention nevertheless dominates the engineering is not arithmetic at all — it is memory: the n² matrix has to exist (§20), and at inference the KV cache has to be read (§25–§26). Those two are what actually bind, and both are bandwidth problems rather than FLOP problems.

The crossover, computed for any configuration you like the FFN width is the surprising control: it moves the crossover and it has nothing to do with attention
The version of this claim that is right. "Attention's cost grows quadratically, so doubling the context more than doubles the cost." True and useful. Doubling n multiplies the linear terms by 2 and the quadratic term by 4, so at Llama-3 8B's 23.5% quadratic share the total goes up by 2 × 0.765 + 4 × 0.235 = 2.47×, not 2×. That is the operationally relevant statement. The one to drop is "attention dominates the cost", which is a statement about a share and is usually wrong.

A note on what "activation memory" includes. The counts above are the attention sub-layer's own tensors. A real training step also holds the FFN's intermediates (n·d_ff per layer, linear in n), the residual stream, the normalisation statistics, and the optimiser state — none of which is quadratic. The training page owns the general activation-memory accounting and gradient checkpointing; this section owns only the term that is quadratic, because it is the only one whose growth is qualitatively different.

21 What attention cannot do (1): it mixes tokens and does nothing to features

Look at where the non-linearity in an attention block actually is. The softmax is non-linear across positions. Along the feature axis, the entire path from input to output is a chain of matrix multiplications — and a chain of matrix multiplications is one matrix.

the value path, written out Y = ∑ⱼ Aⱼ · X · ( W_Vʲ W_Oʲ ) (§14's rewriting) ─ └─────┬─────┘ │ a FIXED (d × d) linear map, the same for │ EVERY position and EVERY input the only place X enters non-linearly, and it does so through Aⱼ, which acts on the POSITION index Hold Aⱼ fixed and the map X ↦ Y is exactly LINEAR. Stack L attention layers with the A's held fixed and you have a product of matrices, which is one matrix. No amount of depth creates a per-token non-linearity. the cleanest special case, because it removes the softmax entirely n = 1. There is one key, so A = [1] whatever the score is. Y = x · W_V W_O , and stacking L such layers gives Y = x · (W_V¹W_O¹)(W_V²W_O²) … (W_Vᴸ W_Oᴸ) = x · M ONE linear map, for any depth. A stack of attention layers applied to a single token is a single matrix multiply. Insert one FFN and it is not.

To be precise about what is and is not being claimed: with more than one token, an attention-only stack is not a linear function of X, because the weights Aⱼ depend on X (§10), and §33's induction circuit is built from attention alone. The claim is narrower and exact — the non-linearity enters only through which positions are mixed, never through a per-feature map, so along the feature axis every head is the fixed linear map W_Vʲ W_Oʲ whatever the input.

This is why the position-wise feedforward sub-layer is not an accessory. The feedforward page's whole argument — that depth plus a point-wise non-linearity is what buys the function class — applies to the feature axis, and attention supplies none of it. The standard division of labour is exactly that: attention moves information between positions; the FFN computes with it. Two-thirds to four-fifths of a block's parameters sit in the second job (§08), which is the arithmetic version of the same statement.

Test the linearity directly: f(αx + βy) against αf(x) + βf(y) stack as many attention layers as you like — with one token the residual stays at round-off; tick the FFN box and it does not
The consequence that gets used. Because the feature-axis map of a head factors through W_Vʲ W_Oʲ, a head's effect on the residual stream is a rank-d_v linear write, and the whole block is a sum of h such writes plus the mixing. That is the structure interpretability work exploits (§33), the structure the rank-collapse theorem of §22 is proved in, and the reason "attention is a communication primitive and the MLP is a computation primitive" is a genuinely accurate slogan rather than a nice-sounding one.

22 What attention cannot do (2): stacked alone, it collapses to one token

Every row of an attention matrix is a probability distribution, so A is row-stochastic, and applying a row-stochastic matrix is averaging. Compose enough averages and everything becomes the same thing. The rate is startling and it is measurable.

the quantity, and why it is the right one res(X) = X − 1·x̄ᵀ x̄ = the column mean, so res removes the best rank-1 "every token identical" part relative residual = ‖res(X)‖ / ‖X‖ 1 = tokens fully distinct 0 = every token identical Averaging is a contraction towards the mean. A stack of pure attention layers is a product of row-stochastic matrices, and the theorem bounds the residual DOUBLY exponentially: log(−log r) grows at least linearly in depth, not log r. Nothing was trained; these are random weights, and that is the point: this is what the operation does, before any learning gets a chance to counteract it.

The discrepancy worth teaching

The usual summary. "Skip connections and MLPs stop the degeneration." Measured with random weights, the MLP on its own makes it worse, and it is the normalisation that does the work. Compare the columns at layer 12: skip alone is at 0.302, skip plus MLP is at 0.120 — the MLP accelerated the collapse — and skip plus MLP plus pre-LayerNorm is at 0.588 and has essentially stopped falling. The reason is scale: a residual branch X + f(X) with an untrained ReLU MLP amplifies whatever component is largest, which is the shared mean, whereas pre-normalisation re-standardises each token before every branch and denies the mean that advantage. The theoretical result is a statement about upper bounds under assumptions about the branch's Lipschitz constant; the measurement is about a specific untrained network. Both are right, and the practical lesson is the second: the pre-norm is load-bearing here, not decorative.
Rank collapse with depth, and the three things that slow it down the token-similarity strip under each curve is the same fact in a different form — watch every row become the same colour
What this does and does not license you to say. It does say that attention has an intrinsic pull towards token uniformity, that the pull is extremely strong when nothing opposes it, and that the residual-plus-normalisation wiring of a real block is what opposes it. It does not say that trained deep transformers collapse in this way — they do not, although a milder version (token representations growing more similar in the later layers) is reported in trained models too. The measurement above uses random weights precisely so that the architectural tendency is visible without the training that counteracts it. The right reading is the same one the RNN page gave for the spectral radius: this is the thing the architecture must be built to survive, and the wiring is how it survives it.

23 What attention cannot do (3): the entropy of a row, and the two ways it fails

One number summarises what an attention row is doing: the entropy of its weights. It has an interpretable maximum, an interpretable minimum, and a well-documented pathology at each end — and it is the cheapest diagnostic there is.

the statistic and its scale H(α) = − ∑ⱼ αᵢⱼ ln αᵢⱼ in NATS maximum ln n uniform: the row is reading everything equally minimum 0 one-hot: the row is reading exactly one key and the number to actually report: effective number of keys read = eᴴ(α) the PERPLEXITY of the row eᴴ is in [1, n] and is directly interpretable: "this row is reading about 4.2 of its 512 available keys". Entropy in nats is not.
failure at the low end
Entropy collapse
Rows become near one-hot everywhere. The softmax Jacobian goes to zero (§06), so those rows stop learning; the layer becomes a hard, brittle router. Empirically it arrives together with training instability — loss spikes and divergence — and the work that named it reports low entropy as a leading indicator: preventing the collapse removed the instability in their runs.
failure at the high end
Dilution
Rows approach uniform. The output is the mean of the values, which carries no positional information at all, and the Jacobian is small again — ‖J‖_F ≈ √(1/n). This is what long context does to a head that has not learned a large enough logit margin (§32).
the healthy middle
A few keys
Trained heads are reported to sit mostly at eᴴ between about 1 and a few dozen, largely independent of n — which is what "attending to a handful of relevant tokens" means quantitatively.

The mechanism behind the low-end failure is a positive feedback loop worth stating, because it explains why it is a training phenomenon rather than an initialisation one. A head that finds a useful key gets gradient that increases that key's logit. Larger logits mean lower entropy. Lower entropy means a smaller softmax Jacobian for every other key in that row, so the alternatives stop receiving gradient. The row commits, and having committed it cannot un-commit. Nothing in the objective penalises this — an attention row has no regulariser of its own — so the only brakes are the ones the architecture supplies.
