07 Query, key, value: a dictionary lookup you can differentiate

Each position produces three vectors by three learned projections: a query (what it is looking for), a key (what it can be found by) and a value (what it hands over when found). A query is scored against every key, the scores become weights, and the output is the weighted sum of the values. The three names come from a data structure — a dictionary — and the analogy is exact enough to be worth spelling out, including the two places it breaks, which is where the interesting design freedom lives.

a hash table, written as arithmetic table { k₁: v₁ , k₂: v₂ , … , k_n: v_n } lookup table[q] = vⱼ where kⱼ = q exact match, or an error as arithmetic: out = ∑ⱼ 1[ kⱼ = q ] · vⱼ └────┬────┘ an indicator: 1 on exactly one j, 0 elsewhere soften the indicator and you have attention out = ∑ⱼ α(q, kⱼ) · vⱼ with αⱼ ≥ 0, ∑ⱼ αⱼ = 1 α = softmax over j of a similarity score. THREE changes, each deliberate: 1 match becomes DEGREE OF MATCH an indicator becomes a weight 2 one result becomes a BLEND the output need not be any stored vᵢ 3 equality becomes SIMILARITY IN A LEARNED SPACE which is what W_Q and W_K are for

Change 3 is the one that carries the load, and it is why the projections exist rather than the raw vectors being used directly.

what the projections buy, in the row convention of §01 Q = X·W_Q (n × dₖ) "what this position is looking for" K = X·W_K (n × dₖ) "what this position offers to be found by" V = X·W_V (n × d_v) "what this position hands over if found" Without them the score would be xᵢ·xⱼᵀ, which is SYMMETRIC and fixed: position i finds j exactly as strongly as j finds i, and the notion of similarity is whatever the embedding happened to produce. With them: score(i,j) = xᵢ W_Q W_Kᵀ xⱼᵀ = xᵢ · M · xⱼᵀ with M = W_Q W_Kᵀ a general BILINEAR form, learned, and NOT symmetric unless M happens to be. "the adjective looks for the noun it modifies" and "the noun looks for its adjective" are different relations, and one matrix expresses both. and the separation of K from V is the other half K decides WHO gets read. V decides WHAT is read. Tying them (K = V) would force the feature that makes a position findable to also be the feature it contributes — so a token could not be easy to find for one reason and useful for a different one. Keeping them separate costs one extra matrix and is part of what lets attention implement "look up the token after the one that matches" (§33): the key says "I follow x", the value says "I am y".

One attention row, by hand

Two places the dictionary analogy genuinely breaks, both of which matter later:

  • There is no "key not found". The weights are a softmax, so they sum to one whatever the scores are. A query that matches nothing does not return nothing — it returns the least-bad blend of everything. §32 shows what that does at long context, and it is the leading explanation of attention sinks: trained models put a large share of many rows' weight on a semantically empty position (typically the first token), and the reading that fits best is that a query with nothing to say needs somewhere to put its unavoidable unit of weight (§29).
  • The rank of M = W_Q W_Kᵀ is at most dₖ. With dₖ < d_model — which is the case in every multi-head configuration in use — the bilinear form is low-rank by construction. A head cannot express an arbitrary relation between positions; it can express one whose matrix has rank at most dₖ. §13 measures what that costs and why it is nevertheless the right trade.
From an exact lookup to attention, one relaxation at a time the temperature slider walks between the two: at the left end the output is one stored value exactly, at the right end it is the plain mean of all of them
The one-sentence version to keep. Attention is a dictionary lookup in which the match is a learned, asymmetric similarity rather than equality, the result is a blend rather than a hit, and every part of it is differentiable — so the model can learn what to store under which key and what to ask for from the downstream loss alone, with no supervision about the lookup itself.

08 Every shape, and the parameter count of one block, exactly

The whole operation in seven lines, with a shape on every line and nothing suppressed. This is the section to come back to when an implementation will not typecheck.

The forward pass, line by line

one multi-head attention block, forward, batch 1, in the layout of §01 shape MACs input X (n × d) — queries Q = X·W_Q (n × h·dₖ) n·d·h·dₖ keys K = X·W_K (n × g·dₖ) n·d·g·dₖ values V = X·W_V (n × g·d_v) n·d·g·d_v ── per head j, with kv = ⌊j·g/h⌋ ────────────────────────────────────── scores Sⱼ = Qⱼ·Kₖᵥᵀ / √dₖ (n × n) n·n·dₖ mask+softmax Aⱼ = softmax(Sⱼ + M) (n × n) — (n² exp) gather Zⱼ = Aⱼ·Vₖᵥ (n × d_v) n·n·d_v ── back together ───────────────────────────────────────────────────── concat C = [Z₁ … Z_h] (n × h·d_v) — project Y = C·W_O (n × d) n·h·d_v·d and the parameters, which do NOT depend on n W_Q d · h·dₖ W_K d · g·dₖ W_V d · g·d_v W_O h·d_v · d total = d·dₖ·(h + g) + d·d_v·(g + h) (biases, if used, add h·dₖ + g·dₖ + g·d_v + d) in the standard case h·dₖ = h·d_v = d and g = h: total = 4 d² four square matrices, and that is all. NOTHING here depends on the sequence length. An attention block has a FIXED parameter count and a sequence-dependent COST — which is the exact opposite of the dense layer of the feedforward page, whose parameter count is fixed by the input size. That is why one trained model runs at any length.

Two things fall out of that table that most descriptions of the architecture do not mention.

  • Attention is a minority of the parameters, in every standard configuration. With a classic 4d two-matrix FFN it is exactly one third — 4d² against 8d². With a three-matrix SwiGLU FFN of the widths modern models use it drops below a fifth. The thing the architecture is named after holds 17–33% of its weights. That is worth remembering whenever a parameter-efficiency argument is made about attention.
  • Grouped-query attention is a parameter saving as well as a cache saving. Going from g = 32 to g = 8 at d = 4096 takes the block from 4d² = 67 108 864 to 41 943 040 — 37.5% fewer, a factor of exactly 1.6. It is usually sold purely as an inference trick (§27); it also makes the model smaller. The saving is exactly (1 − (h+g)/(2h)) of the block.

The reshape that silently corrupts the heads

The shape bug that does not crash. Splitting (n × h·dₖ) into heads with a strided reshape instead of a contiguous one — taking every h-th column for head 1 instead of the first dₖ columns. It runs, it trains, and it is a different grouping of the same columns. Both splits partition the columns, so every shape typechecks and every gradient flows. Trained from scratch the two are equally good — they differ only by a fixed permutation of the columns of W_Q, W_K, W_V and the rows of W_O, so one can be converted into the other exactly. The bug bites when weights trained under one convention are loaded under the other: every head then reads a mixture of the features four different heads learned, quality collapses, and nothing reports it. This page's DL.splitHeads is contiguous, says so, and is the only place the reshape happens.

09 Self-attention and cross-attention: one operation, two wirings

Nothing in §08 required Q, K and V to come from the same tensor. Where they come from is the only difference between the two, and it changes the shapes, the masking and — decisively — what can be cached.

the two wirings, with shapesSELF-ATTENTION one sequence X (n × d) Q = X·W_Q K = X·W_K V = X·W_V S is (n × n) — square, and every position may read every position CROSS-ATTENTION two sequences: X_tgt (n_q × d) and X_src (n_k × d) Q = X_tgt·W_Q K = X_src·W_K V = X_src·W_V S is (n_q × n_k) — RECTANGULAR, and the two lengths are independent Everything between the score and the output is byte-for-byte the same code. §02's decoder reading an encoder is cross-attention — the "encoder–decoder attention" of the original seq2seq models, and the first form to exist. Self-attention came later and is the one that dominates today's models, because it builds a representation of a sequence out of the sequence itself.

The fourth row is the one with practical consequences. In an encoder–decoder model, cross-attention's keys and values are computed once from the source and then reused at every decode step, so the cross-attention cache neither grows nor is recomputed. Self-attention's cache grows linearly with the tokens generated. That asymmetry is why the cache discussion in §24–§28 is a discussion about self-attention, and why an encoder–decoder model with a long source and a short output has a very different memory profile from a decoder-only model given the same text as a prompt. Encoder vs Decoder Models owns that comparison; the Transformer page owns where each block sits in the stack.

10 The framing everything else follows from: a weight matrix computed from the input

An attention head's output is Z = A·V: an (n × n) matrix of weights applied to the values. The point of this section is that A is not a parameter — it is computed from the input, by W_Q and W_K, freshly for every sequence. Written that way, next to the fixed mixing matrix of a dense layer and the banded, tied one of a convolution, the whole series lines up behind one equation.

one head, with the mixing made explicit Z = A · V A = softmax( X W_Q W_Kᵀ Xᵀ / √dₖ ) ─ ─ ─ an (n × n) matrix that MIXES ACROSS POSITIONS, and it is a function of X. Compare, for the same job — producing each output position as a combination of input positions — with the two operators the earlier parts built: dense layer over positions Z = A_dense · V A_dense is a LEARNED PARAMETER: n² free numbers, fixed after training, and its size fixes the sequence length convolution Z = A_conv · V A_conv is BANDED and TIED: f free numbers repeated down the diagonals (the convolution page's Toeplitz matrix), fixed after training attention Z = A(X) · V A is COMPUTED. It has ZERO free parameters of its own; the parameters are in the FUNCTION that produces it, and the matrix itself is different for every input.

Everything the architecture is good and bad at is in that comparison.

  • The parameter count stops depending on the sequence length. A dense mixing matrix over n positions needs n² parameters and can only ever handle that n. Attention's mixing has none — it is produced by W_Q and W_K, which are (d × dₖ) and know nothing about n. That is why the same weights run at 512 tokens and at 128 000.
  • The cost starts depending on the sequence length. The matrix is not stored, so it must be built — n² scores, every forward pass. §18 counts it exactly. This is the price of the previous point, and it is the same trade in both directions.
  • The prior is weak, and it is weak on purpose. A convolution asserts that the useful relations are local and position-independent. Attention asserts almost nothing about which positions should be related, only that whatever the relation is, it should be computable as a low-rank bilinear form on the representations. The inductive-bias table on the feedforward page is organised around exactly this axis, and its transformer row says it in one line: the mixing matrix is computed from the data rather than learned as a fixed parameter. This section is that row, derived.
  • It is a multiplicative operation. A depends on X and then multiplies V, which also depends on X. The output is therefore not linear in the input even before any activation function: the scores XW_QW_KᵀXᵀ are quadratic in X, and they multiply XW_V, so expanding the softmax around uniform gives a linear term (the plain mean of the values) plus a cubic term ∝ (XW_QW_KᵀXᵀ)XW_V, and higher orders after that. A dense layer and a convolution are both linear in their input. That multiplicative interaction is a genuine increase in expressive power per layer, and it is why attention can implement operations like "find the token that matches this one and copy what follows it" (§33) that no fixed linear mixing can.
The same picture for three operators: change the input and see which matrices move the change input button is the experiment — press it and only one of the three mixing matrices is different afterwards
The name for this, and where else it appears. An operation whose weights are produced by a network from its own input is dynamic or input-conditioned; hypernetworks, squeeze-and-excitation gates in convolutional networks, the gates of an LSTM and the router of a mixture of experts are all instances. Attention is the instance where the produced object is an n × n matrix over positions, which is why it is the one that changed how sequences are modelled. Recognising the pattern makes the LSTM's forget gate and attention's weight matrix the same idea at two scales: the network decides, per input, how much of something to let through.

11 Attention does not know about order — proved, measured, and handed over

A convolution knows what "next to" means because its matrix is banded. Attention's matrix is built from content alone, so it has no notion of position whatsoever. That is a two-line proof, it is exactly measurable, and it is the reason the Transformer page has to do something about it.

permutation equivariance of self-attention, in the row conventionLet P be an (n × n) permutation matrix, so PX is X with its ROWS permuted. Q' = (PX)W_Q = P(XW_Q) = PQ and likewise K' = PK, V' = PV S' = Q'K'ᵀ/√dₖ = PQ Kᵀ Pᵀ/√dₖ = P S Pᵀ softmax is applied ROW-WISE and P S Pᵀ permutes rows AND columns by the same permutation, so each row of S' is a row of S with its ENTRIES permuted — and softmax commutes with permuting its own entries: A' = softmax(P S Pᵀ) = P A Pᵀ Z' = A'V' = P A Pᵀ P V = P A V = P Z since PᵀP = I Attention( PX ) = P · Attention( X ) what that means, said as bluntly as it deservesShuffle the words of the sentence, and the OUTPUT IS THE SAME SET OF VECTORS, shuffled the same way. Every downstream position-wise operation — the FFN, the normalisation, the output head — is applied per row and therefore inherits the equivariance. A transformer without positional information is a SET function. It cannot distinguish "dog bites man" from "man bites dog", at any depth, with any number of heads.

Compare with the feedforward page's inductive-bias section, which proved the mirror-image statement for an MLP: permuting the input coordinates leaves an MLP's achievable loss unchanged, because it never knew the coordinates were arranged in a grid. Here the object being permuted is different — positions in a sequence, not features within a vector — but the conclusion has the same shape and the same cause: an operator that treats its inputs as an unordered collection cannot be hurt by reordering them, and being hurt is the evidence that the architecture knew something. A convolution is hurt. Attention is not.

Shuffle the tokens: the output permutes exactly, until a mask or a position signal is added the number to watch is the residual — it is at floating-point round-off for plain self-attention and jumps by fifteen orders of magnitude the moment anything positional is switched on
Three ways out, and this page owns none of them. Position can be added to the input (sinusoidal or learned embeddings), rotated into the query and key before the dot product (RoPE), or added to the scores as a distance-dependent bias (ALiBi and relative-position schemes). All three are architectural choices about how to break the symmetry this section proves, and all three belong to the Transformer page's positional-encoding sections. What matters here is that something must be done, that the requirement is a theorem rather than a heuristic, and that a causal mask is itself a positional signal — the causal-mask option in the figure shows that masking alone breaks the equivariance, which is why decoder-only models are not quite as position-blind as the proof above would suggest.
The overstatement to avoid. "A decoder-only transformer still needs explicit positional encoding, because attention is permutation-equivariant." The premise is right and the conclusion is too strong. A causal mask breaks the permutation symmetry on its own — position i can see i keys and position j can see j, so the positions are already distinguishable — and causal models trained with no positional encoding at all do learn position-dependent behaviour — in most reports somewhat less well than with an explicit scheme, though the gap is task-dependent. The figure measures the symmetry breaking rather than arguing about it. The practical answer is still to add a positional scheme; the reason is quality, not impossibility.
