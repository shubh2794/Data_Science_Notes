Distributed Training

Frontier models don't fit — or don't train in reasonable time — on one device. Distributed training splits the work across many GPUs, by replicating the model, slicing it, or staging it, often all at once.

Deep Learning Scale Systems

01 Two reasons to distribute

  • Throughput: a huge dataset trains too slowly on one GPU — spread the batch to go faster.
  • Capacity: the model (plus its gradients and optimizer state) doesn't fit in one GPU's memory — split it.
  • The three core strategies — data, tensor, and pipeline parallelism — address these and combine into "3D parallelism."

03 Data parallelism

  • Every GPU holds a full copy of the model and processes a different slice of the batch.
  • After the backward pass, gradients are all-reduced (averaged) across GPUs so all copies stay in sync.
  • Simple and the most common — but needs the whole model to fit on each GPU.

04 Tensor & pipeline parallelism

  • Tensor (intra-layer): split a single layer's matrices across GPUs — each computes part of every layer, then combines. Heavy communication; needs fast interconnect.
  • Pipeline (inter-layer): put different layers on different GPUs; micro-batches flow through the stages like an assembly line. Watch out for the idle "bubble" at the ends.
  • These let a model that's too big for one device train across several.

05 Sharding & memory tricks

  • ZeRO / FSDP: shard the optimizer states, gradients, and parameters across data-parallel GPUs instead of replicating them — huge memory savings with data-parallel simplicity.
  • Gradient accumulation: sum gradients over several micro-batches to simulate a large batch on limited memory.
  • Activation checkpointing: drop activations in the forward pass and recompute them in the backward pass — trade compute for memory.

06 Communication is the bottleneck

  • All these strategies move data between GPUs — all-reduce, all-gather, point-to-point — and that traffic, not the math, often limits speed.
  • Real systems combine 3D parallelism (data × tensor × pipeline) plus sharding, tuned to the hardware's interconnect topology.
  • It's a systems problem as much as an ML one.

07 Takeaways

  • Data parallel replicates the model, splits the batch, all-reduces gradients — for throughput.
  • Tensor & pipeline parallel split the model itself (within a layer / across layers) — for capacity.
  • ZeRO/FSDP + accumulation + checkpointing stretch memory further.
  • Communication is usually the real limit; big runs combine all the above.
  • Scales up Neural Network Training; essential for LLMs.
