DL Architectures Compared

A map of the deep-learning zoo. The organizing idea is inductive bias — the structural assumptions each architecture bakes in — which determines what data it fits, how it scales, and how much data it needs.

Deep Learning Architectures Overview

01 One lens: inductive bias

  • Inductive bias = the assumptions an architecture makes about the data. Strong bias → less data needed but narrower fit; weak bias → general but data-hungry.
  • Each architecture matches a data structure: grids, sequences, sets, or graphs.
  • There's no free lunch — the right choice depends on the data and the budget.

04 Choose by data structure

  • Grid (image): CNN, or Vision Transformer at scale.
  • Sequence (text/audio): Transformer (default), SSM for very long contexts, RNN rarely now.
  • Set: Transformer without positional encodings (permutation-invariant).
  • Graph: GNN via message passing.

05 The scaling picture

  • Transformers dominate at scale: weak bias + huge data + parallel training beats hand-built structure — the "bitter lesson."
  • But weak bias costs compute (O(n²) attention), motivating SSMs, sparse/efficient attention, and MoE.
  • Strong-bias models (CNN, GNN) still win when data is limited or structure is known.

06 Takeaways

  • Inductive bias is the organizing principle — strong bias needs less data, weak bias scales further.
  • Match architecture to data: grids→CNN, sequences→Transformer/SSM, graphs→GNN, sets→Transformer.
  • Transformers win at scale via parallelism + weak bias + data; SSMs/MoE address their cost.
  • Ties together the whole Deep Learning domain.
