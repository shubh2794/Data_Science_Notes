Fine-Tuning & Transfer Learning

Don't train from scratch — start from a model that already learned useful representations on a large dataset, and adapt it to your task with far less data and compute.

Deep Learning Transfer Adaptation

01 Why transfer learning

  • Early layers of a trained network learn general features (edges, syntax) that transfer across tasks; later layers are more task-specific.
  • Starting from a pretrained model means you need far less labeled data and compute to reach good performance.
  • It's the default workflow in vision and NLP: pretrain once on a huge corpus, adapt many times.
One sentence: reuse a pretrained model's representations and adapt the parts that need to change for your task.

02 Freeze vs fine-tune

A spectrum from "train only a new head" to "update everything." Slide how many layers you unfreeze.

How much of the network to train Unfreeze more layers for a bigger shift — at more data & compute cost
  • Feature extraction: freeze the backbone, train only a new head — fast, tiny data, smallest shift.
  • Partial fine-tuning: unfreeze the top few layers — adapt task-specific features.
  • Full fine-tuning: update everything — biggest shift, most data and compute, risk of forgetting.

03 Doing it well

  • Lower learning rate than pretraining — you're nudging, not rebuilding.
  • Discriminative rates: smaller LR for early layers, larger for late layers/the head.
  • Warm up the head first (freeze backbone briefly) so a random head doesn't wreck good features.

04 Catastrophic forgetting

  • Aggressive fine-tuning can overwrite the general abilities the base model came with.
  • Mitigations: lower LR, fewer epochs, freeze more, mix in original-task data, or use parameter-efficient methods.
  • It's the central tension: adapt enough to learn the task, not so much you erase the pretraining.

05 The efficient alternative

  • Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters) freezes the base entirely and trains a tiny add-on — most of full fine-tuning's gains, a fraction of the cost, and no forgetting of the frozen weights.
  • For LLMs especially, PEFT is now the default way to specialize a model.

06 Worked example: BERT

  • Pretrain: BERT learns bidirectional representations via masked-language-modeling on a huge corpus.
  • Fine-tune: add a small task head (classification, NER, QA) and train on the labeled task — usually just a few epochs.
  • The same backbone serves dozens of downstream tasks, each a cheap fine-tune. See Encoder vs Decoder Models.

07 Takeaways

  • Transfer learning reuses pretrained representations — less data, less compute, better results.
  • Spectrum: feature extraction (freeze) → partial → full fine-tuning, trading shift for cost and forgetting risk.
  • Use a low LR, warm up the head, and watch for catastrophic forgetting.
  • PEFT is the efficient default, especially for LLMs.
  • Builds on Neural Network Training; specialized form is PEFT.
