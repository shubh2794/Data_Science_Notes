Knowledge Distillation

Train a small, fast student to imitate a large, accurate teacher. The supervision is not the correct answer but the teacher's whole probability distribution — the tiny probabilities it assigns to the wrong classes carry the similarity structure it learned, and that dark knowledge is worth far more per example than a one-hot label.

Deep Learning Compression Efficiency

01 The idea

  • A big model is accurate but slow/expensive; a small model is fast but weaker on its own.
  • Distillation transfers the big model's behaviour into the small one — keeping most of the accuracy at a fraction of the size.
  • The trick: train the student on the teacher's soft probabilities, not just the hard correct label.
One sentence: a small student learns to match a large teacher's output distribution, inheriting its knowledge cheaply.

02 Dark knowledge & temperature

A hard label says "cat." The teacher's softened distribution says "mostly cat, a bit dog, definitely not car" — and that relative structure is the signal. Raise the temperature to reveal it.

Softening the teacher's logits with temperature T Higher T reveals the small probabilities (dark knowledge) the student learns from
soft targets: p_i = softmax(z_i / T) T > 1 softens the distribution

03 The distillation loss

L = α · KL( student_T ‖ teacher_T ) + (1−α) · CE(student, true label)
  • Distillation term: match the teacher's softened distribution (KL divergence at temperature T).
  • Supervised term: also fit the true hard labels, weighted by 1−α.
  • Both teacher and student logits are softened by the same T during training; the gradient is scaled back by T².

04 Why soft targets help

  • The relative probabilities encode similarity structure ("3 looks a bit like 8") that a one-hot label throws away.
  • That richer signal acts like a regularizer and gives the student far more information per example.
  • Result: the student often matches the teacher closely despite being much smaller.

05 Flavours of distillation

TypeWhat the student matches
Response (logit)The teacher's output distribution — the classic recipe
FeatureIntermediate hidden representations, not just outputs
Self-distillationA model teaches a same-size (or future) copy of itself
Data / sequenceFor LLMs: train on teacher-generated text or token distributions

06 Where it's used

  • Shipping small, fast models for on-device or low-latency serving (e.g. DistilBERT).
  • Compressing big LLMs into cheaper ones that retain much of their capability.
  • One tool in the model acceleration kit, alongside quantization and pruning.

07 Takeaways

  • Distillation = student mimics teacher, learning from soft probabilities, not just hard labels.
  • Temperature softens the teacher's logits to expose the "dark knowledge" of relative similarities.
  • Loss blends KL-to-teacher with standard cross-entropy.
  • Variants match outputs, features, or generated data; widely used to compress models.
  • Builds on Neural Network Training; complements Model Acceleration.
