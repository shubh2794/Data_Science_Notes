Deep Learning · part 6 · architecture

Transformers

A Transformer is a stack of identical blocks — an attention sub-layer and a position-wise feedforward network, each wrapped in a residual connection and a normalisation — with token embeddings and positional information in at the bottom and a language-modelling head out at the top. It is built around the attention mechanism, which the attention page owns and which that page ends on two proofs rather than on a summary. The first: self-attention is exactly permutation-equivariant — shuffle the tokens and the output is the same set of vectors, shuffled the same way, measured at 5.6e−16 — so any model that is to know that "dog bites man" differs from "man bites dog" must have position injected, and the mechanism itself says nothing about how. The second: attention mixes tokens and does nothing else — with the weights held fixed the map from input features to output features is one matrix, at any depth — so a per-token non-linearity must be added, and the mechanism says nothing about that either. This page is what happens when you take both proofs seriously. The block has the shape it has because of them: a token-mixing sub-layer and a per-token sub-layer, each wrapped in a residual connection and a normalisation, with position injected once at the bottom or at every attention.

The centre of the page is the architecture as a set of decisions, each derived and each measured: where position goes and what each choice can and cannot extrapolate to (sinusoidal, learned, RoPE, ALiBi, relative buckets, none — trained live and tested past the training length); the residual stream as a shared bus and why the order of the normalisation decides whether the model trains without warmup (the gradient scale through the stack, measured per layer); the position-wise FFN as two-thirds of the parameters and as a key–value memory; the three stacks; the embedding in and the unembedding out, with the √d_model scale and weight tying done right; exact parameter and compute accounting for five real configurations and the 6·N·D rule derived in MACs and in FLOPs; the training recipe as it is actually set — teacher forcing, label smoothing's loss floor, the warmup-then-decay schedule, β₂, clipping, bf16 against fp16; decoding; and a two-layer decoder trained live in the browser from the same primitives every figure uses. The last part of the page follows the same model into a real implementation at inference time: the configuration, the checkpoint on disk and how it is loaded and placed; the attention backends and what each one holds in memory; paged allocation and experts kernels; the KV cache as an object and five strategies for storing it, each with its bytes; the generate loop and its logits processors, stopping and streaming; contrastive search and contrasting layers; strided perplexity; and chat templates — every mechanism with a hand-worked number.

Every number printed here was recomputed before it was printed. Every backward pass on the page — LayerNorm, RMSNorm, the ungated and gated FFN, attention with RoPE and per-head biases, the pre-LN and post-LN block, and the whole tiny model with every positional scheme — was audited against central differences, and §17 prints the worst relative error of each. Three widely repeated claims did not survive the recomputation and are taught as discrepancies in place: that ALiBi is a positional encoding (§09 measures what it can and cannot address), that the quadratic term dominates training compute (§23 counts it for five real models), and that "6ND" is a FLOP count of the weights alone (§23 says exactly what it omits).

01 What a Transformer is, and why the block has the shape it has

One sentence first: a Transformer is a stack of identical blocks, each an attention sub-layer followed by a position-wise feedforward network, with every sub-layer wrapped in a residual connection and a normalisation. Token embeddings, with positional information added to them or rotated into the attention, go in at the bottom; a normalisation and one matrix onto the vocabulary — the language-modelling head — come out at the top; nothing in between changes shape, every block mapping (n × d) to (n × d). Whether the attention is unmasked, causally masked, or followed by a second attention that reads another stack decides the three variants — encoder-only, decoder-only and encoder–decoder — and nothing else about the block differs between them.

the whole model, in the row convention of this page — one TOKEN per ROWtokens t₁ … tₙ integers in [0, V) embed X⁰ = √d · E[t] (+ P) (n × d), one ROW per token; E (V × d); P (n × d) is position if it is ADDED here (§04–§05), absent if it is rotated in inside Attn (§06) or added to the scores (§08) block ℓ = 1 … L, identical in shape, separate in weights, pre-norm form: X₁ = X + Attn( N₁(X) ) token mixing — the ONLY place rows talk to each other Y = X₁ + FFN( N₂(X₁) ) per-token computation — the same MLP on every row Xˡ = Y (n × d) in, (n × d) out; nothing changes shape head logits = N_f(Xᴸ) · W_out (n × V), W_out (d × V), often W_out = Eᵀ (§21) N = LayerNorm or RMSNorm, applied to each ROW independently FFN = act(X·W₁ + b₁)·W₂ + b₂ W₁ (d × d_ff), W₂ (d_ff × d) Attn = multi-head self-attention, and the mask decides the family (§18–§19): encoder-only no mask — every row reads every row decoder-only causal mask — row i reads rows 0 … i encoder–decoder causal self-attention, then a second attention whose keys and values are the encoder stack's output

That is the whole architecture, and every section from §02 on is one of its decisions taken apart and measured. The block has exactly these two sub-layers, and not one, because of two theorems about the mechanism — both handed over by the attention page rather than resolved there. Each one forces a component. Put the two components together with the only wiring that lets them stack, and the Transformer block is what you get.

Why the block has two sub-layers: the two hand-offs from the attention page

Restate the two results exactly as the attention page's permutation-equivariance section and its token-mixing section leave them, because the whole architecture is a response to them.

hand-off 1 — attention is permutation-equivariant, quotedfor any (n × n) permutation matrix Π, Attention(Π·X) = Π·Attention(X) (Π, not P — P is the positional matrix everywhere on this page) so a stack of attention layers, each followed by any PER-ROW operation (a norm, an FFN, an output head), is a SET function of its tokens: it cannot tell "dog bites man" from "man bites dog" at any depth. Measured residual on the attention page's figure: 5.6e−16 — round-off. Position is not "weak" in the mechanism; it is ABSENT, and must be injected. hand-off 2 — attention mixes tokens and does nothing to features, quotedY = ∑ⱼ Aⱼ · X · (W_Vʲ W_Oʲ) with the Aⱼ held fixed, X ↦ Y is LINEAR with one token (n = 1) there is nothing to mix, A = [1], and L stacked attention layers are ONE matrix: Y = x · ∏ₗ (W_Vˡ W_Oˡ). No depth of attention creates a per-token non-linearity. One must be added.

From the two hand-offs to the block

Hand-off 2 says a block needs a second sub-layer that acts on each token's feature vector alone — a small MLP applied to every row, the same weights at every position, which the feedforward page already owns and which §14 names the position-wise feedforward network. Hand-off 1 says something about position has to enter, and in every scheme in wide use it enters at one of three places: added to the input rows before the first block, rotated into the queries and keys inside every attention, or added to the score matrix as a distance-dependent bias. §03 lays the three out and §04–§10 take each apart. What neither hand-off says, and what turns two sub-layers into a trainable stack of dozens of them, is the wiring: every sub-layer adds its output to its input rather than replacing it, and every sub-layer's input is normalised first. The residual makes depth trainable (§11); the normalisation's position decides whether the model needs warmup (§12). That is the whole block, and it has not changed since 2017 except in the order of those two operations, in the choice of norm, activation and positional scheme, and in the attention variant inside the first sub-layer (grouped-query and multi-latent heads, both on the attention page).

the block, pre-norm form, in the row convention of the attention page — one TOKEN per ROWinput X (n × d) d = d_model, one row per token X₁ = X + Attn( N₁(X) ) token mixing: hand-off 1's object, plus position Y = X₁ + FFN( N₂(X₁) ) per-token computation: hand-off 2's repair N = LayerNorm or RMSNorm, applied to each ROW independently Attn = multi-head self-attention, exactly the attention page's DL.mhaForward, causal mask or not FFN = act(X·W₁ + b₁)·W₂ + b₂ W₁ (d × d_ff), W₂ (d_ff × d) every operation is either PER ROW (N, FFN) or ACROSS ROWS (Attn); nothing is both, and that separation is the architecture's one real idea.

Every quantity on this page obeys the attention page's layout law and nothing here re-declares it: a sequence is X (n × d) with one token per row, a weight is (d_in × d_out) and sits on the right, heads split the last axis into adjacent blocks, masks are additive and −∞. Symbols: n is always the number of tokens, L the number of blocks (the scalar loss is also written L, as on every other page of this series; a superscript or a product, xᴸ, 12·L·d², is the layer count, and ∂L/∂· is the loss), d = d_model, h heads, g key/value heads, dₖ = d/h, d_ff the FFN width, V the vocabulary. Two consequences are new to this page and both are places where a derivation silently flips convention: RoPE rotates rows — each row of Q and of K is rotated, pair of columns by pair of columns, by an angle that depends on that row's position, and V is never touched (§06) — and in the FFN the "keys" of the memory reading are the columns of W₁, because a column of W₁ is what a token row is dotted with, while the "values" are the rows of W₂ (§16). Both are written on rows below and both are checked in code.

02 The block, sub-layer by sub-layer, with a real matrix pushed through it

§01 wrote the block in two lines. This section pushes an actual (n × d) matrix through those two lines, prints what every intermediate looks like, and names the one template every sub-layer obeys — because once the template is visible, pre-norm against post-norm, LayerNorm against RMSNorm and gated against ungated FFNs are each a one-symbol edit.

The sub-layer template

Both halves of the block are instances of one pattern. A sub-layer is a function f from (n × d) to (n × d); it is wrapped as x ↦ x + f(N(x)) in the pre-norm form or as x ↦ N(x + f(x)) in the post-norm form. The residual addition is what lets f be small — at initialisation f(N(x)) has a norm comparable to x's or smaller, so the block starts close to the identity and depth costs nothing to optimise through (§11). The norm is what keeps the input to f at a fixed scale whatever has been accumulated on the stream below it. Neither operation has anything to do with attention; they are the same two operations a deep residual CNN uses, in the same roles.

the two wirings, side by side — the ONLY difference between them is where N sitsPRE-NORM (the form nearly every large model since 2019 uses; "pre-LN" when the norm is LayerNorm) X₁ = X + Attn(N₁(X)) Y = X₁ + FFN(N₂(X₁)) the residual path X → X₁ → Y is an UNBROKEN sum: Y = X + Attn(·) + FFN(·) POST-NORM (the 2017 form; "post-LN") X₁ = N₁(X + Attn(X)) Y = N₂(X₁ + FFN(X₁)) the residual path passes THROUGH a norm twice per block: the stream is re-scaled after every sub-layer, and the identity path is not an identity a pre-norm stack of L blocks needs ONE more norm at the very top, N_f, before the output head — because the last thing written to the stream was never normalised. A post-norm stack does not.

What the trace shows

The figure below builds one block from DL.blockInit — the attention page's attention block plus this page's FFN and norms — and pushes a seeded random X through it, recording the row norms and one token's feature vector at every stage. Three things are visible at once. First, in pre-norm wiring the norm of the stream grows through the block: ‖Y‖ > ‖X₁‖ > ‖X‖ for a typical row, because two things were added and nothing rescaled the sum; §11 measures the growth across a whole stack. Second, in post-norm wiring the output rows all have the same norm — √d at unit γ — whatever the input was; the wiring erases the stream's scale twice per block. Third, and easy to miss, the FFN's contribution is larger than attention's at initialisation for the default weight scale, which is a consequence of its width: d_ff = 4d hidden units each contributing to every output coordinate. Both wirings compute a legitimate function; what differs is what the gradient sees on the way back, and that is §12.

Three things to notice in the numbers. Row 0 of A is [1, 0]: with the causal mask the first token can attend only to itself, so its attention output is exactly its own value vector, and the block's first sub-layer is, for that token, a fixed linear map — the n = 1 case of the attention page's token-mixing proof, in the flesh. Row 1 blends the two values with weights 0.668 / 0.332, set by the scores 0.4 and −0.3 through the softmax. And both output rows are longer than the input rows — 4.48 and 3.65 against 2.45 — because two things were added and nothing rescaled the sum; the second N₂ normalised only what the FFN read, not the stream. Post-norm wiring on the same matrices would end with every row at norm 2.

Trace one block: row norms at every stage, and one token's vector as a strip switch the wiring and watch the output row norms — pre-norm lets them grow, post-norm pins every one to √d; the weight scale slider is the initialisation, and it changes how much each sub-layer writes relative to what it reads
The description to avoid. "The residual connection passes the input around the sub-layer so the network can skip it if it wants." It is an addition, not a bypass, and the sub-layer cannot be skipped. Y = X + f(N(X)) always includes f's output; what the residual guarantees is that the identity is always present in the sum, so that ∂Y/∂X = I + ∂f/∂X has a term of norm 1 no matter how small ∂f/∂X is. That is a statement about the gradient, and §11 measures exactly that Jacobian. A gate that could set f's contribution to zero is a different design — a highway layer — and the Transformer block does not have one.

35 What the architecture cannot do, and the bugs that do not crash — measured

Two lists, deliberately separate. The first is what the architecture cannot do by construction, each with the page that owns the remedy. The second is the set of implementation errors that produce a running, training, plausible-looking model that is not the model you meant — and for four of them a measurement that catches the error in one line.

What it cannot do

cost
Quadratic attention
Every layer forms an (n × n) matrix per head; the attention page's cost sections count it and its FlashAttention section shows the memory traffic can be fixed without the arithmetic changing. At the trained context it is a minority of the compute (§23); at 8× the context it is two-fifths of the total for a 7B-class model and the majority for a 125M-class one.
state
No recurrence, no compressed state
The "state" at inference is the whole KV cache, 2·L·d per token, growing without bound. A recurrent model carries a fixed dₕ; the RNN page showed what that costs in fidelity. The architectures that bring a fixed state back with a better recurrence are State Space Models.
length
A trained context
Every positional scheme degrades past the length it was trained at (§09) and one cannot run at all (§05). Extending it is a procedure, not a property — Context-Length Extension.
compute
Fixed compute per token
Every token gets exactly L blocks whether it needs them or not: the same arithmetic for "the" and for the answer to a puzzle. Conditional compute inside the block is Mixture of Experts; more compute per answer at inference is Reasoning in LLMs.
precision
Exact algorithms
A fixed-depth stack computes a fixed circuit class; tasks that need unbounded serial steps (long arithmetic, deep parity) are not in it at any width without scratch tokens. The empirical side of this is Large Language Models'.
data
A weak prior
The attention page's section on the input-computed weight matrix: the mixing matrix has no locality, no translation equivariance, no built-in structure. That is why the architecture needs the data volume it needs, and why a CNN still wins small-data vision (Vision Transformers).

The bugs that do not crash

wiring
Pre-norm stack, no final norm
The logits scale with ‖xᴸ‖, which grows with depth (§11). The figure measures the logit standard deviation against L with and without ln_f. §11.
wiring
Wiring and schedule mismatched
Post-norm at a pre-norm's peak rate stalls; pre-norm at a post-norm's rate converges slowly. Nothing throws. §12.
embedding
The √d scale forgotten (or doubled)
With a N(0, 1/d) table and no factor the position term is √(d/2)× the token term and the first layer's scores ignore the tokens (§20, correlation ≈ 1); with a N(0, 0.02²) table the factor is unnecessary and harmless. Know which table you have. §20.
head
Tied weights with a unit-variance table
Logits √d too large at initialisation; the loss starts in the hundreds instead of at ln V and the first steps are spent undoing it. §21 measures it.
mask
Padding mask applied after the softmax
Rows stop summing to one, the output is rescaled by the unmasked mass — by a different factor per row. The figure measures the row sums. The attention page's masking section; figure below.
mask
Padding mask on the query axis
Creates all-masked rows; with −∞ that is a NaN (loud); with −1e9 it is a uniform row attending to everything (silent). The figure shows both. The attention page's −∞-against-a-large-negative section.
position
RoPE applied to V
The output acquires absolute position; a common shift of every position changes the output by order one instead of round-off. The figure measures the shift residual both ways. §06.
position
RoPE pairing convention swapped
Adjacent against rotate-half: both have the offset property, both run, and a checkpoint trained under one is a different model under the other. §06.
FFN
Keys and values transposed in the memory reading
Reading the rows of W₁ as keys in the row convention. Names the wrong vectors; nothing computes differently. §16.
recipe
Weight decay on norms and biases
The gains are pulled to zero every step; the model fights its own regulariser. §27.
recipe
β₂ = 0.999 with spiky gradients
Overshoot at the spike and a starved step for the next thousand. §27 measures it.
recipe
Reporting the smoothed loss as a perplexity
A floor of 1.41 nats at ε = 0.1, V = 50k, is not a perplexity of 4.1; the unsmoothed loss is the comparable number. §25.
units
MACs reported as FLOPs
Every estimate off by exactly two. §23.
mask
A float mask in the 1 / 0 convention
An additive kernel adds 0 to the forbidden entries and +1 to the allowed ones: the future stays visible, held-out loss improves, generation collapses. §41.
template
Wrong chat format, or a doubled BOS
The model answers in any format; quality drops. Rendering and then tokenising with special tokens added starts the sequence <s><s>. §50.
generation
End-of-turn token not in the stop set
The model ends its turn, then writes the user's next message. §47, §50.
cache
A per-layer quantity read globally
A cache sized from the global key/value head count is wrong for every overridden layer of a heterogeneous stack. §40.
cache
A cached prefix reused against a re-rendered history
The template dropped or changed a token of the earlier turns; the cache no longer matches the prompt. §44.
loading
A module replaced after loading
The new layers were never filled from the checkpoint: fluent nonsense. §36.
loading
A fused weight split naively across devices
One device gets all of the gate, the other all of the up projection; shapes match, outputs are wrong. §39.
evaluation
Perplexity without its stride
The same model and text score 19.44 or 16.44 depending on the window. §49.

52 What to remember, and exactly where the remaining parts begin

The architecture, in the order it was built

  • The block is a response to two theorems. Attention is permutation-equivariant, so position must be injected; attention mixes tokens and does nothing to features, so a per-token non-linearity must be added (§01). A token-mixing sub-layer, a per-token sub-layer, a residual around each, a norm before each: that is all of it (§02).
  • Position enters through one of three doors — added to the input, rotated into Q and K, or added to the scores (§03). The sinusoidal table's shift is a linear map (§04, measured at 2e−13); the learned table cannot be indexed past its length (§05); RoPE makes the score depend on the offset alone (§06, 5e−15) with a spectrum of clocks the base controls (§07); ALiBi is a recency prior per head (§08) and cannot address an exact offset (§09); a causal mask alone leaks 1/(i+1) (§10).
  • Measured past the training length: RoPE degrades gently, ALiBi barely, sinusoidal fast, learned not at all — it stops (§09).
  • The residual stream is a bus. Every sub-layer adds; nothing overwrites; the norm grows like L^0.4; a pre-norm block's Jacobian is near the identity and a post-norm block's is not (§11). Post-norm gives every layer roughly the same gradient scale and a lower learning-rate ceiling; pre-norm's top-layer gradient shrinks as L^−0.35 and tolerates the larger rate (§12). RMSNorm is LayerNorm without the mean, and the mean does not appear to be load-bearing (§13).
  • The FFN is two-thirds of the block at ratio 4 and more under grouped-query attention (§14); gated units hold the count at ⅔ width and the rounding rule produces 11 008, 14 336, 28 672 (§15); the FFN is a key–value memory whose keys are the columns of W₁ (§16). Every backward pass is audited to 3e−7 or better (§17).
  • The stacks differ by a mask (§18), plus a third sub-layer for the encoder–decoder (§19). The embedding's √d is a scale-matching device whose necessity depends on the table's initialisation (§20); the head is a third of a small model and 1% of a large one, and tying it makes the untrained model predict its own input (§21).
  • Counting: six places for parameters, five closed forms (§22); 6·N·D is 3·N·D MACs plus an attention term of n/12d relative size (§23).
  • The recipe: teacher forcing through the mask gives every position's loss in one pass (§24); label smoothing sets a floor of 1.41 nats and a logit gap of 13.0 (§25); warmup then decay, with the peak scaling as (d·warmup)^−½ (§26); β₂ = 0.95 because 0.999 overshoots a spike 3× and starves the next thousand steps (§27); bf16 because gradients underflow fp16 without a loss scale (§28).
  • Decoding reshapes one distribution (§29) or searches over sequences (§30); on a solved task all rules agree. Depth against width at fixed budget barely matters within 40 < d/L < 130, and the toy's result is the depth floor, not the plateau (§31). The model trained live is 4 672 parameters and finds the copy circuit at offset −4 (§32); the logit lens reads it arriving after block 1 (§33). Three families, one budget, four numbers each (§34); six things it cannot do and twenty-one bugs that do not crash (§35).

The model at inference, in a real implementation

  • A model is three objects: a configuration that fixes every shape (the 8B-class count, 8 030 261 248, follows from eight fields), a base model with swappable task heads, and a preprocessor (§36). On disk it is sharded safetensors plus an index, the configuration, the tokenizer and its chat template — 16.06 GB and four 5 GB shards for that model in bf16 (§37).
  • Loading should use the stored dtype, build the skeleton on the meta device so the weights are never held twice (peak ≈ M, not 2M), and fill the fastest device first a whole block at a time; weights spilled to the CPU are streamed every step, about 130× slower than reading them from GPU memory (§38). Checkpoint and runtime layouts differ, and reversible conversions — renames, chunks, expert stacking, the RoPE permutation of §06 — run as the bytes arrive (§39). In a heterogeneous stack, compute every per-layer quantity per layer (§40).
  • Attention backends compute the same function; eager holds h·n² scores (2 GiB per layer at 4k, 128 GiB at 32k for §36's model) and a flash-style kernel about 80 MiB and 644 MiB; masks must be in the format the kernel expects, and a 1 / 0 float mask silently shows the future (§41). Paged allocation cuts cache waste from a reservation's 60–80% to under a block per sequence (§42). A mixture-of-experts layer is computed by a grouped matmul for prefill and a batched one for decode (§43).
  • The cache is a per-layer list with one update call, and its mask, position ids and length must agree (§44). Dynamic, static, offloaded, sliding and quantised strategies hold 250 MiB, 512 MiB, 16 MiB, 250 MiB and 80 MiB for a 2 000-token conversation on the 8B model — trading memory against compile-friendliness, bus traffic and dequantisation (§45).
  • Generation is prefill, then one step at a time through a fixed pipeline of logits processors — penalties, bans, then §29's warpers — whose repetition penalty is not shift-invariant (§46); stopping and streaming must work on text rather than ids, and a step costs ≈ 16 ms of prefill arithmetic per 1 000 prompt tokens and ≈ 4.8 ms of weight reading per output token (§47). Contrastive search, contrasting layers, and diverse and constrained beams each add one term to the choice (§48).
  • Perplexity depends on the evaluation window: 13.20, 12.04 and 11.54 for strides 6, 3 and 1 on the toy, 19.44 against 16.44 on a real model (§49). Chat templates serialise messages into the exact format a model was trained on, special tokens included, once (§50). The deployment levers — precision, a fused kernel, and GQA-and-RoPE-era architecture — are §51.
