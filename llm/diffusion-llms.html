Diffusion LLMs

Most language models generate left-to-right, one token at a time. Diffusion (discrete) language models instead start from noise/masked tokens and iteratively denoise the whole sequence in parallel — a different bet on how to decode text.

LLMs & Generative AI Generative Non-autoregressive

01 A different way to decode

  • An autoregressive LLM emits token t only after t−1 — generation is inherently sequential, so latency grows with output length.
  • A diffusion LM holds the entire output and refines all positions together over a few denoising steps, decoding many tokens per step.
  • It borrows the idea from image diffusion — learn to reverse a corruption process — but adapts it to discrete tokens.
One sentence: generate text by iteratively denoising a whole masked sequence in parallel, instead of predicting one next token at a time.

02 Discrete diffusion

Continuous noise doesn't make sense for tokens, so the corruption is masking or replacement.

  • Forward process: progressively corrupt the text — typically masking tokens (absorbing-state diffusion) until the sequence is all [MASK].
  • Reverse process: a Transformer learns to predict the original tokens from the corrupted sequence, recovering text over several steps.
  • Training resembles a scaled-up masked-language-modeling objective across many corruption levels.

03 Parallel iterative decoding

04 Tradeoffs

StrengthCost

Any-order generation; natural infilling & editingNumber of steps trades speed vs quality
Global view of the sequence while decodingHarder to combine with KV-cache tricks
Controllable, can revise earlier tokensLess mature tooling/ecosystem

The appeal is latency: if you can produce a good sequence in, say, 8 steps instead of 200 sequential tokens, decoding is far faster.

05 Landscape

  • Research lines: masked/absorbing discrete diffusion, and continuous diffusion over embeddings.
  • Recent text diffusion models show competitive quality with far fewer decoding steps, and several labs have shipped diffusion-style LLMs for fast generation.
  • Closely related: non-autoregressive and parallel-decoding methods (and speculative decoding aims at the same latency goal from the AR side).

06 Takeaways

  • Diffusion LLMs decode by denoising a whole masked sequence in parallel, not token-by-token.
  • Discrete diffusion corrupts via masking; a Transformer learns the reverse (unmask) process.
  • Parallel + any-order gives low latency, easy infilling, and revisable tokens — at some quality and tooling cost.
  • Conceptual cousins: image diffusion and non-autoregressive decoding.
