Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning

Fully fine-tuning a billion-parameter model for every task is expensive to train and to store. PEFT freezes the pretrained weights and trains a tiny set of new ones instead — often <1% of the model — for nearly the same quality.

LLMs & Generative AI Fine-tuning Efficiency

01 Why not just full fine-tuning?

Full fine-tuning works, but it scales badly the moment you have more than one task.

  • Compute & memory: updating every weight needs optimizer state and gradients for all parameters — often the real bottleneck is GPU memory, not flops.
  • Storage: a separate full-size copy of the model per task. Ten tasks = ten × the weights.
  • Catastrophic forgetting: aggressively retraining all weights can erode the general capability the base model came with.

PEFT's bet: keep the pretrained weights frozen and learn a small adapter that nudges the model toward the task. You store only the tiny adapter per task.

One sentence: freeze the giant model, train a few new parameters, get most of full fine-tuning's quality at a fraction of the cost.

02 LoRA: low-rank adaptation

The dominant PEFT method. Don't update W — add a low-rank correction BA beside it. Drag the rank.

h = W·x + (B·A)·x W frozen (d×d), B (d×r), A (r×d), r ≪ d trainable params: 2·d·r instead of d²
Full fine-tuning vs LoRA (per d×d weight matrix, d=1024) The rank r sets how many parameters you actually train
  • A is initialized random, B to zero — so the adapter starts as a no-op and training only adds what helps.
  • At inference you can merge BA into W, so LoRA adds zero latency once merged.
  • Typical ranks are tiny (4–64), yielding well under 1% trainable parameters.

03 Why low rank works

The update a task needs is far simpler than the model itself.

  • Empirically, the change in weights during fine-tuning (ΔW) has low "intrinsic rank" — it lives in a small subspace.
  • So approximating ΔW ≈ B·A with a small r captures most of the useful adaptation while ignoring directions that don't matter.
  • The pretrained model already holds the capability; fine-tuning just needs to steer it, which is a low-dimensional move.

05 QLoRA: quantize the base

Push the memory win further by storing the frozen model in 4-bit.

  • The frozen base weights are quantized to 4-bit (NF4), slashing memory, while LoRA adapters train in higher precision on top.
  • Gradients flow through the quantized weights to the adapters — the base never updates, so its low precision is fine.
  • Result: fine-tune very large models on a single GPU, with quality close to 16-bit full fine-tuning. See also Model Acceleration for quantization.

06 Serving many adapters

One base model, many tiny task adapters — a big operational win.

  • Keep one copy of the base in memory and swap LoRA adapters per request/task — megabytes each, not gigabytes.
  • Merge an adapter into the base for zero-overhead serving of a single task, or keep them separate to multiplex many tasks.
  • This is what makes per-customer / per-task customization economical at scale.

08 Takeaways

  • PEFT = freeze the base, train a tiny adapter — most of full fine-tuning's quality, a fraction of the cost and storage.
  • LoRA adds a low-rank delta BA beside frozen W; 2dr params instead of d², mergeable for zero inference cost.
  • Low rank works because the fine-tuning update is intrinsically low-dimensional.
  • QLoRA quantizes the frozen base to 4-bit so big models fine-tune on one GPU.
  • One base, many adapters — cheap per-task customization at serving time.
  • Subset of fine-tuning & transfer learning; pairs with LLMs.
