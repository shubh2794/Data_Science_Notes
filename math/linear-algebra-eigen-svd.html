Eigendecomposition & SVD

The two great factorizations. Eigenvectors are the directions a matrix only stretches; the SVD extends that picture to every matrix of any shape — rank, subspaces, condition number, pseudoinverse, and low-rank compression all read straight off it. Complex numbers come first because rotations have complex eigenvalues.

The notes, in 2 parts

Two parts, one per factorization. Part 1 takes square matrices apart along the directions they only stretch: the complex numbers that rotations force on us, then eigenvalues, diagonalization and its failure modes, the spectral theorem and the variational view. Part 2 is the SVD, the factorization every matrix has, and everything that reads straight off it: rank, the four subspaces, norms, low-rank approximation, conditioning and the pseudoinverse.
