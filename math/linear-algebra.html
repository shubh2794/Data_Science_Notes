why The thread that runs through it

One idea keeps recurring: a matrix transforms space, and almost every question is about which directions survive that transformation and which collapse.

dot product→matrix multiplication→rank→four subspaces→solvability of Ax = b→det & inverse→projection→least-squares→eigenvectors→SVD→definiteness→covariance→PCA
  • Algebra and geometry together. Every operation gets a formula and a picture. The dot product is a sum of products and a cosine; a determinant is a cofactor expansion and a volume; an eigenvector is a null-space computation and an unrotated direction.
  • Rank is the hinge. It fixes the size of all four subspaces, decides whether a system has zero, one, or infinitely many solutions, whether an inverse exists, and how many singular values are non-zero.
  • The Gram matrix AᵀA is everywhere. It is square and symmetric for any A, positive semidefinite, shares the rank and row space of A, drives the normal equations and the left inverse, its eigenvalues are the squared singular values of A, and for centered data it is the covariance matrix.
  • Theory vs practice. The concept-level tools (determinant, explicit inverse, cofactor expansion) are for understanding; numerical work uses row reduction, QR, and the SVD with a tolerance for "zero".
