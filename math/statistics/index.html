why The thread that runs through it

Every technique in this subject is a variation on one move: compare what you observed against what chance alone would have produced, and measure the gap in units of the variability you would expect anyway.

a sample→a statistic→its sampling distribution→standard error→interval or p-value→a claim about the population
  • The standard error is the unit of measurement. A confidence interval is an estimate plus or minus a few standard errors; a test statistic is a distance divided by a standard error; an F ratio is one variance divided by another. Learn to read every formula as "observed effect, measured in units of noise".
  • Variability shrinks like 1/√n, not 1/n. Four times the data halves the standard error. This single fact sets the cost of precision, explains diminishing returns in data collection, and is why the central limit theorem is the load-bearing result of the subject.
  • Design outranks analysis. No test rescues a biased sample. Randomisation is what converts an association into a causal claim, and nothing computed afterwards can substitute for it.
  • The same question, two answers. A confidence interval and a two-sided test at the matching level always agree: the interval contains the null value exactly when the test fails to reject. Prefer the interval — it reports an effect size, not just a verdict.
  • Assumptions are the price. Every closed-form result buys its formula with an assumption — normality, independence, equal variances, large counts. Resampling pays the price in computation instead, which is why it is the honest fallback when the assumptions look shaky.
