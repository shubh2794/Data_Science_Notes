GPT

Generative Pre-trained Transformer — the decoder-only, autoregressive model family (GPT-1, GPT-2, GPT-3, InstructGPT/ChatGPT, GPT-4) that turned "predict the next token" into a general-purpose interface. This note follows the family itself: the objective, the exact block each generation used, the published configurations and their parameter and compute arithmetic, how each generation was adapted to tasks, how text is actually decoded, and what the later reports do and do not disclose. Where BERT reads bidirectionally to produce representations, GPT reads left to right to produce text.

01 What GPT is

GPT is a stack of causally masked Transformer decoder blocks trained to maximise the probability of each token given the tokens before it. Every model in the family uses that same objective; the generations differ in scale, normalisation placement, tokeniser, data, and — above all — in how the trained model is pointed at a task.

the objective every GPT is pretrained onP(x₁, …, xₙ) = ∏ᵢ P(xᵢ | x₁, …, xᵢ₋₁) minimised as next-token cross-entropy, parameters θℒ(θ) = − (1/n) ∑ᵢ log P_θ(xᵢ | x₍<ᵢ₎)

The shape of the computation

  • Input: a sequence of n token ids. Each id is looked up in a token-embedding table, a learned position vector is added, and the result passes through L identical blocks of masked self-attention plus a position-wise feed-forward network.
  • Output: at every position i, a vector of |V| logits — a full distribution over the next token. During training all n distributions are scored at once; during generation only the last one is used, a token is chosen, appended, and the model runs again.
  • There is no encoder and no cross-attention. The same stack reads the prompt and writes the continuation, which is why the family is called decoder-only (see Encoder–Decoder Models for the other two wirings).

Why the family matters

  • GPT-1 (2018) showed generative pretraining followed by supervised fine-tuning transfers well; GPT-2 (2019) showed a larger model does many tasks zero-shot if the task is phrased as text; GPT-3 (2020) showed few-shot in-context learning from examples placed in the prompt; InstructGPT (2022) showed a small amount of preference-based post-training makes the model follow instructions; GPT-4 (2023) added image input and reported predicting its final loss from runs using up to 10,000× less compute.
  • The architecture changed remarkably little across those five steps. Most of the story is scale, data, and the adaptation interface.
Takeaway: GPT is a causal Transformer decoder trained on next-token cross-entropy. One objective, one block design, and a sequence of increasingly cheap ways to adapt it: fine-tune (GPT-1), prompt zero-shot (GPT-2), prompt with examples (GPT-3), and instruction-tune with human preferences (InstructGPT).

30 Limitations of the GPT design

Some limitations come from the objective, some from the architecture, and some from scale. Each has its own page; the GPT-specific view is summarised here.

From the objective

  • Hallucination. Next-token prediction rewards plausible continuations, not true ones. GPT-3's closed-book QA and InstructGPT's closed-domain hallucination rates (41% for GPT-3 on the paper's API prompts) are direct measurements. Detection and mitigation: Hallucination & Factuality.
  • Frozen knowledge. Everything the model knows comes from a corpus with a cutoff (December 2017 links for WebText; September 2021 for most of GPT-4's data). Retrieval is the usual remedy: Retrieval-Augmented Generation.
  • Bias. The model reproduces associations in its data — GPT-3's occupation and religion probes, and InstructGPT's lack of improvement on bias benchmarks.

From the architecture

  • Hard context ceiling of 512, 1024 or 2048 tokens for GPT-1/2/3, fixed by the learned position table; and quadratic attention cost in context length. Remedies: Context-Length Extension.
  • Left-to-right only. Tasks that need comparing two passages or revising earlier output (WiC, ANLI, DROP for GPT-3) are harder in a single causal pass. Multi-step prompting and reasoning methods partly compensate: Reasoning in LLMs.

From scale

  • Cost. 3.14 × 10²³ FLOPs to train GPT-3 and 349 GB of 16-bit weights to serve it; the GPT-3 paper itself notes generating 100 pages costs on the order of 0.4 kWh. Serving economics: LLMOps.
  • Evaluation contamination. Web-scale training data overlaps benchmarks; the GPT-2, GPT-3 and GPT-4 reports each include an overlap analysis, and none can rule it out.
  • Opacity. From GPT-4 on, the architecture and data are not public, so external claims about why the model behaves as it does cannot be checked against its design.
Takeaway: the objective explains hallucination and bias, the architecture explains the context ceiling and weakness at comparison tasks, and scale explains cost and contamination. Fixing one layer rarely fixes the others.

31 Pitfalls

Mistakes that recur when reasoning about, reproducing, or using GPT-family models.

When reading or quoting the papers

  • Quoting "117M" and "345M". The code repository itself calls them errors; the checkpoints are 124M and 355M. Use the formula in §12 when a number matters.
  • Assuming GPT-3 used dense attention in every layer. The paper says alternating dense and locally banded sparse — without the details, which is why exact reproduction is impossible from the paper alone.
  • Citing a GPT-4 parameter count. The report gives none; any number is from outside the report and should be labelled as such.
  • Comparing perplexities across tokenisers. Per-token perplexity depends on how many tokens a text becomes. Compare bits per byte or per character instead, as GPT-2 did for enwik8 and text8.
  • Treating one few-shot number as the model's ability. Example choice and order can move accuracy by tens of points; report means over several prompts.

When implementing or serving

  • Off-by-one targets. Logits at position i predict token i+1; shift labels by one and drop the last logit. Forgetting the shift trains a copy model with near-zero loss.
  • Porting pre-LN weights without the final LayerNorm, or applying post-LN order to GPT-2/3 weights. Outputs degrade badly, often into repetition or noise, without any error being raised.
  • Exceeding the position table. GPT-2 has no row 1025; silently truncating or wrapping positions produces garbage beyond the limit.
  • Packing documents without the end-of-text separator. GPT-3 relies on it (not a mask) to tell unrelated documents apart.
  • Underestimating the KV cache. At GPT-3 scale it is 4.5 MiB per token per sequence at 16 bits; a large batch of long contexts can exceed the weights' size.
  • Expecting temperature 0 to be bit-reproducible. Greedy decoding is deterministic in exact arithmetic, but batched floating-point kernels on accelerators often are not, so ties and near-ties can flip between runs.
Takeaway: most GPT mistakes are bookkeeping — a shifted label, a missing LayerNorm, a mislabelled size, an unfair comparison — rather than deep modelling errors. The formulas on this page catch most of them.

33 What to remember

Ten statements that summarise the family, each backed by a section above.

Ten statements

  • One objective. Every GPT is trained on next-token cross-entropy under a causal mask; the chain rule makes this an exact likelihood model.
  • Parallel training, sequential generation. The mask gives n training targets per pass and makes the KV cache valid, but generation needs one dependent step per token.
  • A nearly fixed block. GELU, learned positions and a tied output from GPT-1; pre-LN, a final LN and 1/√N residual init from GPT-2; alternating banded sparse attention in GPT-3.
  • Byte-level BPE with 50,257 entries arrived with GPT-2 and was kept by GPT-3.
  • Arithmetic you can check. 12·L·d² + V·d reproduces the reported sizes (and exposes the 117M label); 6ND gives GPT-3's published 3.14 × 10²³ FLOPs (the paper computed it the same way).
  • The interface changed each generation: fine-tune with input transformations (GPT-1), write the task as text (GPT-2), add demonstrations (GPT-3), align to preferences (InstructGPT).
  • In-context learning is a robust behaviour with an unsettled mechanism and high prompt sensitivity; "emergence" is at least partly a property of the metric.
  • GPT-3 was undertrained by later compute-optimal standards — about 1.7 tokens per parameter against roughly 20.
  • Post-training beat scale for user preference: 1.3B InstructGPT over 175B GPT-3, for under 2% of pretraining compute.
  • GPT-4 is documented by capabilities, not design. Quote only what its report states.
Takeaway: GPT is the clearest case in modern ML of one simple objective, scaled with care and adapted through ever-lighter interfaces, turning into a general-purpose system. Understanding the arithmetic and the evidential status of each claim matters more than memorising benchmark scores.
