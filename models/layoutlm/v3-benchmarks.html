18 v3: linear patches instead of a CNN

LayoutLMv3 removes the convolutional backbone. The page image is resized to 224 × 224, cut into 16 × 16-pixel patches, and each patch is flattened and linearly projected to the model width — the ViT recipe. The v3 paper describes itself as the first multimodal document AI model that does not rely on a pre-trained CNN or Faster R-CNN for image features.

patch count for an H × W image and patch size PM = H·W / P² = 224·224 / 16² = 14 · 14 = 196 image embedding: linear projection plus learnable 1-D position embedding per patchxₘ = W_patch · flatten(patchₘ) + Pos1D_img(m) W_patch ∈ ℝ^(D × 3·16·16) text embedding: RoBERTa word embedding plus 1-D and segment-level 2-D positionsyₗ = Tok(wₗ) + Pos1D(l) + Layout(x̂₀, ŷ₀, x̂₁, ŷ₁, ŵ, ĥ of wₗ's segment) the encoder sees text then image; 1-D and 2-D relative biases as in v2input = [ y₁ … y_L ; x₁ … x_M ]

Worked example: what the patches cost

The projection is a 16 × 16 convolution with stride 16 from 3 to 768 channels: 3 · 16 · 16 · 768 weights + 768 biases = 590,592 parameters, the "0.6M" the v3 paper reports for adding image embedding. The v3 paper calls this negligible next to CNN backbones, citing 44M for a ResNet-101; v2's ResNeXt-101-FPN is about 90M. The Transformers implementation also prepends a visual [CLS] token and learns a 197-row position embedding (196 patches + [CLS]), so the image contributes 197 tokens to the sequence.

A word's corresponding patches follow from its box: "Total:" at (665, 826, 745, 843) maps to 224-pixel coordinates x 148.96–166.88 and y 185.02–188.83, i.e. patch columns floor(148.96/16) = 9 to floor(166.88/16) = 10 in patch row floor(185.02/16) = 11. Those two patches are what word–patch alignment checks.

Patchify, mask, and label word–patch alignment resample the masks · change the image mask ratio · click a word

The mock invoice is resized to 224 × 224 (aspect ratio is not preserved, as in the reference processor) and split into 14 × 14 patches. Image masking follows BEiT's blockwise scheme: rectangles of at least 16 patches with aspect ratio between 0.3 and 1/0.3 are added until the ratio is reached. Words are masked independently at the text ratio. A non-masked word is labelled aligned if none of the patches its box overlaps is masked, unaligned otherwise; masked words are excluded, as in the paper. The paper does not spell out the overlap rule; "any overlap" is this figure's assumption.

Other v3 architecture choices

  • Segment-level layout for text tokens (sub-words); image patches get their grid cell as a box, and special tokens get zeros.
  • RoBERTa initialisation and its byte-level BPE vocabulary (50,265 entries) replace BERT/UniLMv2 WordPiece; the maximum text length stays 512 (the configuration lists 514 positions because RoBERTa's position ids start after the padding index).
  • 1-D position embeddings for patches: the authors "have not observed improvements from using 2-D position embeddings in [their] preliminary experiments".
  • Training stability: attention computed in the CogView form, softmax(QᵀK/√d) = softmax( (QᵀK/(a√d) − max(QᵀK/(a√d))) · a ) with a = 32, which rescales before subtracting the maximum.
Takeaway: v3 spends 0.6M parameters on image input where v2 spent about 90M, and gets the image into the sequence at 196 patch tokens — more visual tokens than v2's 49, each looking at a quarter of the area.

19 v3 pretraining: MLM, MIM and word–patch alignment

v3's argument is that text and image objectives should look alike. Text uses masked language modelling over discrete word tokens; image uses masked image modelling over discrete visual tokens produced by an image tokenizer; and word–patch alignment ties the two by asking whether a word's image patches were masked. The total loss is the plain sum.

MLM: mask 30% of text tokens in spans, lengths from Poisson(λ = 3); layout keptℒ_MLM = − ∑_(l ∈ L′) log p( yₗ | X_masked , Y_masked ) MIM: blockwise-mask about 40% of patches; predict each masked patch's visual token id (8,192-way)ℒ_MIM = − ∑_(m ∈ M′) log p( zₘ | X_masked , Y_masked ) WPA: for each unmasked text token, aligned / unaligned (two-layer MLP head)ℒ_WPA = − ∑_(l ∉ L′) log p( aₗ | X_masked , Y_masked ) ℒ = ℒ_MLM + ℒ_MIM + ℒ_WPA

The image tokenizer

MIM's targets come from a discrete VAE image tokenizer, the approach of DALL·E and BEiT. The v3 paper initialises its tokenizer from the one in DiT (arXiv 2203.02378), a self-supervised document image Transformer; the DiT paper trained that dVAE on the 42 million page images of IIT-CDIP because the DALL·E tokenizer, trained on natural images, reconstructed documents poorly. It has an 8,192-entry codebook and downsamples by 8, so a 112 × 112 input — the released configuration's second_input_size — would yield a 14 × 14 grid of codes, one target per 16 × 16 patch of the 224 × 224 image (the v3 paper itself does not state the tokenizer's input size). The tokenizer is used only to make labels and is not counted in the model's parameters.

Worked example: one sequence

With 512 text tokens, 30% masking hides about 154 tokens in spans averaging 3 tokens — roughly 51 spans. With 196 patches, 40% masking hides about 78 patches in rectangular blocks. WPA is then computed on the roughly 358 unmasked text tokens. If "Total:" is unmasked but patch (11, 10) is masked, its WPA label is unaligned; if both (11, 9) and (11, 10) are visible, aligned.

A 512-token sequence; span lengths are drawn from Poisson(λ) with a seeded generator, zero-length draws are redrawn (an assumption — implementations differ), and spans are placed at random non-overlapping starts until the ratio is reached (the last span is clipped). Adding patches without an image objective (#2) helps FUNSD (+0.75) but hurts CORD and RVL-CDIP and makes layout-detection fine-tuning diverge; MIM restores CORD and RVL-CDIP and makes PubLayNet trainable (while FUNSD dips by 0.20), and WPA improves every column. Takeaway: v3's objectives are symmetric — discrete tokens reconstructed on both sides — plus an alignment label that falls out of the masking for free. Without MIM, the cheap linear patches help FUNSD slightly but hurt the other tasks and cannot be fine-tuned for layout detection.

20 v3: configurations, recipes and results

Base: 12 layers, 12 heads, width 768, feed-forward 3,072. Large: 24 layers, 16 heads, width 1,024, feed-forward 4,096. Both pretrained on 11 million IIT-CDIP pages — of the roughly 42 million pages the collection splits into — with no image augmentation. The paper reports 133M and 368M parameters.

On PubLayNet (validation mAP @ IoU 0.50:0.95, image only) LayoutLMv3-base with Cascade R-CNN scores 95.1 overall — text 94.5, title 90.6, list 95.5, table 97.9, figure 97.0 — against 94.5 for DiT-base in the same framework; the largest gain is on titles, which the authors attribute to language pretraining since titles are small and identifiable by content.

Reading the table fairly

  • FUNSD: v3 and StructuralLM use segment-level boxes, the rest word-level; the v3 paper itself says the two "are not directly comparable". Part of the jump from 82.76 to 90.29 may be the box granularity rather than the architecture.
  • DocVQA: v3-large (83.37) is slightly below v2-large (83.48) at equal train-only data; the paper marks the models that score higher (TILT, StructuralLM) as using more DocVQA training data and "expected to score higher".
  • Parameters: the 133M includes pretraining heads (parameter counts); the deployable encoder is about 125M.
Takeaway: v3 beats v2 on every task in the table except large-size DocVQA (83.37 vs 83.48), with fewer parameters and no detector, and is the first family member that doubles as a layout-detection backbone. Its FUNSD lead is real but partly protocol.

21 Parameter counts, recomputed from the configurations

The papers give rounded parameter counts, and several of them differ from what the released configurations add up to — some explained by pretraining heads, two (LayoutLM-large and LayoutXLM-base) not explained by the papers. Recomputing from the configuration files — vocabulary, width, depth, table sizes, backbone — shows where each count comes from and what the reported numbers include.

a BERT-style encoder with a pooler (vocab V, positions P, segment types T, width d, depth L)N_enc = V·d + P·d + T·d + 2d + L·(12d² + 13d) + (d² + d) v1 layout tables (R = 1024 rows): the paper's two, or the implementation's fourpaper: 2·R·d implementation: 4·R·d (X, Y, H, W) v2 / v3 layout slices and relative-bias tables (h heads)R·(2c + 2s) with 4c + 2s = d biases: 32·h + 2·64·h

Worked example: LayoutLM-base

V = 30,522, P = 512, T = 2, d = 768, L = 12. Word embeddings 30,522 · 768 = 23,440,896; 1-D positions 393,216; segments 1,536; embedding LayerNorm 1,536; twelve layers at 12·768² + 13·768 = 7,087,872 each, 85,054,464 in all; pooler 590,592. That is BERT-base's 109,482,240. The paper's two 1024 × 768 layout tables add 1,572,864 for 111,055,104; the implementation's four add 3,145,728 for 112,627,968 — the "113M" the paper reports sits closer to the latter, and closer still (113.25M) if the masked-LM head is counted.

Every group is computed in the script from the released configuration values (Hugging Face Hub config.json for each checkpoint) with the formulas above. The ResNeXt-101 32×8d FPN backbone is counted as its convolution weights (86,539,456) plus the four lateral and four output FPN convolutions (3,344,384), with batch-norm affine terms treated as frozen buffers as in Detectron2's default; counting them as trainable adds 202,880. The v3 head sizes are inferred: a dense + LayerNorm + 8,192-way linear MIM head and a two-layer MLP WPA head reproduce the paper's 6.9M and 0.6M at base size to the precision stated (6,891,776 and 592,130), and the same shapes are assumed at large size.

Two numbers that are often misquoted

  • LayoutLM-large on FUNSD is 0.7789 in its own paper (11M pages, 1 epoch) and 0.7895 in the v2 paper's rerun. The v3 table uses 77.89.
  • DocVQA "0.73 → 0.87" mixes settings: 0.7295 in the v2 abstract matches no number in the paper's tables and is most plausibly a transposition of LayoutLM-large's 0.7259 (every other starting value in that abstract is a LayoutLM-large figure), 0.7259 is LayoutLM-large fine-tuned on train only, and 0.8672 is LayoutLMv2-large fine-tuned on train + dev after question-generation pre-fine-tuning. The like-for-like large comparison is 0.7259 → 0.8348.
Takeaway: within the family, each generation improves on the last on almost every task at equal settings; v3's margins are largest on FUNSD (with the segment-box caveat) and smallest on RVL-CDIP and DocVQA.
