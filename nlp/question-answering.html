Question Answering

From "where is the answer in this passage?" to "what is the answer?" — span extraction, retrieval, and generation.

01 The QA landscape

"Question answering" spans wildly different systems. They differ on one axis: where the answer comes from — a given passage, a corpus, or the model's own weights.

  • Extractive (reading comprehension): the answer is a span of a provided passage. The model points to start and end tokens. (SQuAD.)
  • Open-domain: no passage given — retrieve relevant documents from a corpus, then read them. The retriever-reader pipeline, the backbone of RAG.
  • Generative / closed-book: an LLM answers from its parameters alone, generating free-form text — fluent but ungrounded.
  • Multiple-choice: pick the correct option among candidates (a classification framing).

02 Extractive QA: span prediction (SQuAD)

The canonical setup: given a question and a passage, predict the start and end token positions of the answer span. A reader built on BERT over contextual Embeddings.

  • Encode [CLS] question [SEP] passage [SEP]. For each passage token, produce two scores: how likely it begins the answer, how likely it ends it.
  • Two learned vectors s (start) and e (end) dot with each token's hidden state, then softmax over positions gives start/end distributions.
  • The predicted span is the (i, j) pair with the highest combined score subject to j ≥ i (the end can't precede the start).
Pstart(i) = softmaxi( s · hi ) Pend(j) = softmaxj( e · hj ) span(i, j) = s · hi + e · hj , maximize over j ≥ i

Answerability & abstention

  • SQuAD 2.0 adds unanswerable questions: the right move is to abstain, not guess.
  • Mechanism: compare the best span score to a no-answer score (often the [CLS] position). Answer only if the span beats the null by a margin.
  • Abstention connects to Textual Entailment — a candidate answer should be entailed by the passage; otherwise the model should decline.

03 Start/end distributions & span selection

The span isn't a single classification — it's the argmax over a pair of distributions. Seeing both curves makes the j ≥ i constraint concrete.

  • The reader outputs a start probability and an end probability for every token.
  • The chosen span maximizes Pstart(i) · Pend(j) over valid pairs (j ≥ i, span length capped).
  • Sharp, confident peaks → a trustworthy answer; flat distributions → the model is unsure and may need to abstain.

Switch questions: the start (purple) and end (orange) distributions shift, and the constrained argmax selects the answer.

04 Open-domain: retriever-reader

Most real questions arrive without a passage. Open-domain QA first finds relevant text, then reads it — the architecture that grew into RAG.

  • Retriever: encode the question and every passage into Embeddings; fetch the nearest passages by vector similarity. Dense retrieval (DPR) trains the encoders so questions land near their answering passages.
  • Approximate search: with millions of passages, exact search is too slow — an ANN index (HNSW) inside a Vector Databases (HNSW) store gives sub-linear retrieval.
  • Reader: an extractive or generative model produces the answer from the retrieved passages, often fusing several (fusion-in-decoder).
  • Hybrid retrieval: combine dense (semantic) with sparse BM25 (lexical) to catch both paraphrase and exact-term matches.
retrieve: top-k passages by sim( q-emb, p-emb ) read: answer = Reader(question, passages)
Failure shifts upstream: if the retriever misses the passage holding the answer, even a perfect reader fails. In open-domain QA, retrieval recall is the ceiling on accuracy.

05 Closed-book QA & evaluation

An LLM can answer from memory alone — and how you score answers depends on whether they're spans or free text.

Closed-book / generative

  • The model generates the answer from its parameters, no retrieval. Convenient, but ungrounded — the prime setting for hallucination.
  • Grounding it with retrieval (RAG) trades a little latency for a large drop in fabricated answers.

Evaluation

  • Exact Match (EM): 1 if the predicted string equals a gold answer after normalization (lowercase, strip articles/punctuation), else 0. Strict.
  • Token-level F1: treat predicted and gold answers as bags of tokens; reward partial overlap — more forgiving than EM.
  • Abstention: on unanswerable questions, the correct prediction is "no answer"; scored as a correct class, not a span.
EM = 1[ normalize(pred) = normalize(gold) ] token-F1 = 2 · precision · recall / (precision + recall) over shared tokens
Cross-link: EM and token-F1 are part of the broader NLP Evaluation Metrics story; free-text generative answers increasingly use embedding-based and LLM-judge scoring covered there.
