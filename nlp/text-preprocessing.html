Text Preprocessing

Before text reaches a model it's usually cleaned and normalized. Classical NLP did a lot of this by hand; modern sub-word pipelines do far less — but knowing the steps explains both eras.

NLP Pipelines Fundamentals

01 Why preprocess

  • Raw text is messy — mixed case, punctuation, encodings, whitespace — and naive models treat "Cat", "cat," and "cat" as different.
  • Preprocessing reduces noise and vocabulary size so the signal is easier to learn.
  • How much you do depends on the model: classical bag-of-words/TF-IDF needs heavy cleaning; modern LLMs want almost none.

03 Normalization

  • Casing: lowercasing collapses "The"/"the" — but loses signal for NER (proper nouns).
  • Unicode & accents: normalize encodings (NFKC), optionally fold accents.
  • Whitespace, numbers, URLs: standardize or replace with placeholders.

04 Stop-words, stemming & lemmatization

  • Stop-word removal: drop very common words ("the", "is", "of") that carry little topical signal — useful for retrieval, risky for tasks where they matter.
  • Stemming: crude suffix stripping ("running" → "run", "runs" → "run") — fast but can mangle words.
  • Lemmatization: map to dictionary form using morphology ("better" → "good") — slower, more accurate.

05 Weighting terms: TF-IDF

After cleaning, classical pipelines need to weight terms — not all words are equally informative. TF-IDF rewards words frequent in a document but rare across the corpus.

  • Term frequency tf(t, d) — how often term t appears in document d. Common words score high here.
  • Inverse document frequency idf(t) — how rare t is across the N documents. A term in every doc (like "the") has df ≈ N, so idf → 0.
  • Multiplying them down-weights stop-words and ubiquitous terms automatically and up-weights the distinctive ones — a statistical alternative to a hand-built stop-word list.
tf-idf tf-idf(t, d) = tf(t, d) · idf(t) idf idf(t) = ln( N / ( 1 + df(t) ) )

The 1 + in the denominator is smoothing — it avoids division by zero for unseen terms and softens the weight of near-universal ones.

TF-IDF over a toy corpus Bars sorted by tf-idf — high = distinctive to this doc, low = common across the corpus

Notice "the" sits near zero in every document (it appears everywhere, so idf ≈ 0), while topical words rise to the top — which is exactly the down-weighting that stop-word removal does by hand.

06 The modern shift

  • Transformer LLMs use sub-word tokenization (BPE) directly on near-raw text — no stemming, stop-word removal, or lowercasing.
  • The model learns casing, morphology, and punctuation as signal rather than discarding it.
  • So heavy preprocessing largely belongs to classical/TF-IDF pipelines; for LLMs, "preprocessing" is mostly cleaning, dedup, and quality filtering at the dataset level.

07 Takeaways

  • Preprocessing reduces noise and vocabulary — normalize, optionally remove stop-words, stem/lemmatize.
  • Lemmatization > stemming for accuracy; stemming is faster.
  • TF-IDF weights terms by tf · idf — common words fade, distinctive ones stand out.
  • Match it to the model: heavy for classical/TF-IDF, minimal for sub-word LLMs.
