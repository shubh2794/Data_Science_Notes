12 What sampling does, in one picture

Sampling looks like throwing information away, and that is the wrong mental model. What sampling actually does is replicate the spectrum, endlessly, at intervals of the sampling rate. Nothing is lost while the copies stay apart. Everything is lost, irrecoverably, the moment they overlap.

a sampled signal is the original times a comb of impulsescomb(x; Δ) = ∑ₘ δ(x − mΔ) spikes every Δ f_s(x) = f(x) · comb(x; Δ) sampling = MULTIPLICATION and the transform of a comb is a comb, with the spacing invertedF{ comb(x; Δ) } = (1/Δ)·comb(ω; 2π/Δ) Tight in space ⇒ loose in frequency. This is the scaling property of §04 applied to a shape that is its own transform apart from that inversion. so, by the multiplication property of §04, sampling CONVOLVES the spectrum with a combF_s(ω) = (1/2π)·( F ∗ (1/Δ)·comb(ω; 2π/Δ) ) = (1/Δ)·∑ₘ F(ω − m·2π/Δ) Convolving with a comb means COPY-AND-PASTE. The spectrum of the sampled signal is the original spectrum, repeated forever, at spacing ω_s = 2π/Δ.
This is the same fact as "the DFT is periodic in k", from §06. There it was proved in one line from e^{−j2πn} = 1; here it is derived from the comb. Both say: discrete in space ⟺ periodic in frequency. And §03's Fourier series said the dual: periodic in space ⟺ discrete in frequency. The DFT is the case where both hold, which is why it is a finite object with a finite frequency axis that wraps.

The three regimes, and what makes them different

f_s > 2·f_max
Oversampled
The copies are separated by a gap. The original spectrum can be cut out with an ideal low-pass filter and the signal recovered exactly. This is not "good enough" — it is exact, and §13 gives the reconstruction formula.
f_s = 2·f_max
Critically sampled
The copies just touch. Still recoverable in principle, but the reconstruction filter must be an ideal brick wall with no transition band at all, which is a sinc of infinite extent. Critical sampling is a limit, not a design point.
f_s < 2·f_max
Undersampled — aliased
The copies overlap and add. In the overlap region, the sum of two different contributions is stored as one number, and there is no operation that separates a sum into its two parts. The damage happens at the moment of sampling and no subsequent filter, model or network undoes it.

13 The sampling theorem, and the reconstruction it promises

The theorem is usually quoted as an inequality and remembered as a rule of thumb. It is neither: it is an exact reconstruction formula, and the inequality is the condition under which that formula converges to the right answer.

the statementIf F(ω) = 0 for all |ω| ≥ ω_max, and the sampling rate satisfies ω_s > 2·ω_max equivalently f_s > 2·f_max, then f is determined EXACTLY by its samples f(nΔ), Δ = 2π/ω_s. and the formula that reconstructs it — Whittaker–Shannon interpolationf(x) = ∑ₙ f(nΔ) · sinc( π(x − nΔ)/Δ ) sinc(u) = sin(u)/u The sinc is the ideal low-pass filter of §05, and this sum is precisely "cut out one copy of the replicated spectrum and throw the rest away". The interpolation kernel is not a design choice here; it is FORCED by the requirement of exactness. why it works, in two linesThe sampled spectrum is ∑ₘ F(ω − mω_s). If the copies do not overlap, then multiplying by a box of half-width ω_s/2 leaves EXACTLY F(ω) and nothing else. Multiplying by a box in frequency is convolving with a sinc in space. Done.
Three things the theorem does not say, each of which is asserted somewhere as though it did.
· It does not say "sample at twice the highest frequency". It says strictly more than twice. Sampling a sinusoid at exactly twice its frequency can give you all zeros — try sin(πn). The equality case fails.
· It does not say anything about non-band-limited signals. If the hypothesis is false the conclusion is not "approximately true"; there is no error bound, and §15 is about what actually happens.
· It does not say the reconstruction is practical. The sinc is infinitely wide and decays as 1/x, so a truncated sinc is not an approximation with a small error — it is a different filter, whose error is a windowing question (§21) rather than a sampling one.

Nyquist in pixels

translating the theorem into the units an image actually useslattice spacing = 1 pixel ⇒ f_s = 1 sample per pixel ⇒ Nyquist frequency = 0.5 cycles/pixel = ω = π The FINEST structure a pixel grid can carry is one full cycle every TWO pixels: one light pixel, one dark pixel, repeating. Anything finer aliases. A "1-pixel-wide line" is therefore already at the very edge of what the grid can represent, and a half-pixel-wide one is not representable at all — it is stored as something else, and the something else depends on where it happened to fall.

14 Aliasing: where a frequency goes when it is too high

An aliased frequency is not lost and it is not attenuated. It is relabelled, as a specific, computable, lower frequency, and once relabelled it is indistinguishable from a genuine signal at that lower frequency. The relabelling has a formula and the formula is worth knowing exactly.

the fold rule, in cycles per sampleA sinusoid of true frequency f, sampled at rate 1, is recorded as f_apparent = | ( (f + ½) mod 1 ) − ½ | always in [0, ½] The frequency axis is folded, accordion-fashion, at every multiple of ½. measured: 200 samples, the peak bin of the DFT, against the rule true f rule says measured peak bin agreement 0.05 0.0500 10/200 = 0.0500 exact 0.30 0.3000 60/200 = 0.3000 exact 0.40 0.4000 80/200 = 0.4000 exact 0.45 0.4500 90/200 = 0.4500 exact 0.55 0.4500 90/200 = 0.4500 exact ← 0.55 and 0.45 0.70 0.3000 60/200 = 0.3000 exact are the SAME 0.80 0.2000 40/200 = 0.2000 exact samples 0.95 0.0500 10/200 = 0.0500 exact Note the pairs. f = 0.45 and f = 0.55 do not merely look similar after sampling; they produce the SAME NUMBERS. There is no algorithm, no prior, no network that can tell them apart from the samples, because the samples are identical.

The three faces of the same fact

Where you meet itWhat it looks likeThe fold, spelled out
A fine texture photographed too smallMoiré: broad curved bands that are not on the object at all, and that move when the camera does.A grating at 0.7 cycles/pixel folds to 0.3. The recorded pattern is a real, sharp, low-frequency grating — which is why it looks convincing and why Part 2 §28's moiré is so hard to dismiss as noise.
A wheel in a filmRotating backwards, or standing still while the car moves.Frames sample the angle. A spoke advancing 340° per frame is indistinguishable from one retreating 20°: the fold maps +340° to −20°. At exactly 360° per frame the apparent rate is zero and the wheel freezes.
A downsampled image with no pre-filterJagged, crawling edges and invented detail that is sharper than the original.Decimating by r halves the Nyquist limit r times over. Content between the new and old limits does not vanish — it folds down into the surviving band and adds contrast that was never there. Part 2 §22 measured that contrast going above the reference.
Aliasing is not blur, and the confusion is expensive. Blur removes high frequencies. Aliasing moves them, into the low band, at full strength. So an aliased image is often sharper than a correctly filtered one — it has more contrast at fine scales — and looks better on a first glance and worse on every subsequent measurement. Any pipeline judged by an eyeball sharpness test will select the aliased option. This is the reason the pre-filter of Part 2 §22 is mandatory rather than advisory.

15 The honest version: nothing you photograph is band-limited

The sampling theorem has a hypothesis, and for images that hypothesis is false. An occlusion boundary is a step; a step has a 1/ω spectrum that never terminates. So the theorem does not apply, there is no rate at which sampling becomes lossless, and every pre-filter is a trade rather than a solution. This section states the trade and measures it.

why an image cannot be band-limitedOne object in front of another produces a DISCONTINUITY in radiance. §05: a step has a spectrum decaying as 1/ω, extending to infinity. A band-limited function is ANALYTIC — knowing it on any interval determines it everywhere. An image with an occlusion boundary manifestly is not: what is behind the occluder is not determined by what is in front of it. So band-limited images do not merely fail to occur; they are the wrong model.

What actually band-limits an image, and only partially

Three physical stages attenuate high frequencies before the lattice sees them, and Part 1 §18 and §25 derive all three. Their combined effect is the point spread function, and its transform is the modulation transfer function — the honest, physical version of "the pre-filter".

StageIts transfer functionWhat it does and does not fix
Diffraction & defocusAn Airy pattern or a blur disc; its transform is a smooth roll-off that reaches exactly zero at the diffraction cutoff.This is the only genuinely band-limiting stage — beyond the cutoff, the optics transmit nothing at all. But the cutoff is usually above the pixel Nyquist limit in a modern sensor, which is precisely why aliasing is a live problem.
Pixel area integrationA box of the fill-factor width; its transform is a sinc, with the first zero at 1/(fill width).Attenuates, but does not eliminate: the sinc's side lobes pass energy at every frequency, alternating in sign. A 100% fill factor gives a sinc with its first zero exactly at the sampling frequency — helpful, and still not band-limiting.
Optical anti-alias filterUsually a birefringent plate that splits each ray into a 2 × 2 spot: a 2-tap box, transform cos(πf/f_s).A deliberate, tuned blur that trades resolution for alias suppression. Many cameras now omit it and accept aliasing in exchange for sharpness — an explicit engineering choice, and a defensible one, but a choice.

What this means for the rest of the series. Every scale-space construction, every pyramid, every stride-2 convolution and every "resize to 224 × 224" is sitting on this table. Part 2 §23's binomial pre-filter is a specific row of it, chosen because it is cheap and its response is non-negative. A stride-2 convolution layer in a convolutional network is the same operation with the pre-filter learned — and nothing in the loss function requires the learned filter to suppress the doomed band, so networks routinely alias and the aliasing shows up as texture-dependent instability under small translations. Anti-aliased downsampling is a known and effective fix, and it is this table applied.
