22 Colour: three numbers for an infinite-dimensional thing

Light arriving at a pixel is a whole function of wavelength. Three numbers come out. Colour science is the study of what survives that collapse, and its central discovery is that three is not a limitation of cameras but a fact about eyes — which is exactly why colour reproduction is possible at all.

Trichromacy

The human retina has rods, used at low light levels and giving no colour at all, and three types of cone: S (short, blue-ish), M (medium, green-ish) and L (long, red-ish). There are roughly 6 million cones, almost all within a few degrees of the fovea, and roughly 100 million rods spread across the rest of the retina — which is why peripheral and night vision are sharp in neither detail nor colour, and why looking slightly to one side of a faint star makes it visible.

what a cone class actually computesresponse = ∫ L(λ) · S(λ) dλ an INNER PRODUCT of the spectrum with a fixed sensitivity curve three classes ⟹ three inner products ⟹ a projection of an infinite-dimensional space onto ℝ³so infinitely many spectra give identical responses. Such pairs are METAMERS. This is the entire basis of colour reproduction: a screen with three primaries need not reproduce a spectrum, only its three projections. and the failure mode metamerism guaranteestwo fabrics that match under daylight need NOT match under tungsten, because changing L(λ) changes the three integrals by different amounts. Metamerism is illuminant-dependent.
The additive/subtractive confusion, resolved. Red + green light gives yellow because the two together excite the L and M cones in the same ratio that a true 570 nm yellow does — no new wavelength is created; the eye simply cannot tell the mixture from the pure line. Additive primaries (red, green, blue) add emitted light and are what a display does. Subtractive primaries (cyan, magenta, yellow) remove wavelengths by absorption and are what ink does. They are different operations on different quantities, and the schoolroom claim that "blue and yellow make green" is about paint, not light.

So the set reachable from three primaries is exactly the TRIANGLE they span — and since the locus is convex and curved, no triangle inscribed in it can cover it. A three-primary display CANNOT reproduce every visible colour, and adding a fourth primary enlarges but never completes it.

The payoff is that Euclidean distance in L*a*b* approximates perceptual difference, so "these two colours look about equally different as those two" becomes a subtraction. The cost is that the space is non-linear, so averaging in L*a*b* is not averaging light — anything that models physics (blending exposures, simulating a blur, compositing) must be done in linear XYZ or linear RGB, and only then converted for display or for measuring a difference.

23 Gamma: the non-linearity in every image you will ever load

A stored pixel value is not proportional to the light that made it. It is roughly proportional to the light raised to the power 1/2.2. This is the single most consequential fact about image data for anyone doing physics with it, and it is invisible until it bites.

Where it came from, and why it stayed

the historical cause: a cathode-ray tube's brightness was a power law in its input voltageB = V^γ with γ ≈ 2.2 so the camera pre-compensated, and the two cancelled end to endY′ = Y^(1/γ) at the camera Ŷ = (Y′)^γ = Y at the display typical 1/γ = 0.45 the accident that made it outlive the hardwarenoise added in transmission was ADDED to Y′ and then SQUASHED by the display's exponent in the dark regions, where the eye is most sensitive to relative differences. Gamma encoding is, accidentally, a near-optimal perceptual quantisation — so it survived the death of the CRT and is baked into every 8-bit format in use.

Why it is the right thing to do anyway

The eye responds to relative luminance differences — roughly 1% is the discrimination threshold across a wide range. A linear 8-bit encoding spends its codes uniformly in luminance, which means enormous relative steps in the shadows and wastefully fine ones in the highlights.

The bug that is everywhere. Resizing, blurring, or blending images in gamma-encoded values. Averaging V is not averaging light: ((a^(1/γ) + b^(1/γ))/2)^γ ≠ (a + b)/2. A 50/50 blend of black and white in 8-bit sRGB gives code 128, which is 21.6% of white in linear light, not 50%. The visible symptoms are downsampled images that look too dark, antialiased text that looks too thin, and Gaussian blurs whose bright details lose energy. Anything simulating a physical process — §18's defocus, §19's light transport, exposure blending, demosaicing — must linearise, operate, re-encode. Anything perceptual — a histogram, a difference metric, a display — is fine as it is.

24 Colour filter arrays: two thirds of every colour image is invented

A silicon photosite counts photons; it has no idea what colour they were. Three-chip cameras exist and are expensive. Almost every camera instead puts a mosaic of coloured filters over one sensor, measures one channel per pixel, and interpolates the other two — so two thirds of the numbers in an RGB image were never measured.

The Bayer pattern

the 2 × 2 tile that tiles the whole sensor G R G R 50% green B G B G 25% red G R G R 25% blue B G B G why green is doubled, and it is not arbitraryluminance is Y = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B — green carries 72% of perceived brightness, and the eye's spatial acuity for LUMINANCE far exceeds its acuity for CHROMINANCE. So the channel that most needs spatial resolution is sampled twice as densely as the others. The same asymmetry reappears in §25's compression as 4:2:0 chroma subsampling. what a single pixel actually knowsone number. The other two channels are DEMOSAICED — estimated from neighbours. Every RGB image from a single-sensor camera is 1/3 measurement and 2/3 interpolation.

Demosaicing, and the artefacts each method leaves

method
Nearest / bilinear
Average the available neighbours of the same colour. Cheap, and wrong at every edge: it produces zipper artefacts — alternating light and dark pixels along a high-contrast edge, because the interpolation stencil straddles the edge differently on the two phases of the mosaic — and false colour, coloured fringes where a purely luminance edge is split unevenly between the channels.
method
Constant hue / colour ratio
Interpolate R − G and B − G rather than R and B. The colour differences are far smoother than the channels themselves across an edge — an edge is mostly a luminance event — so interpolating them and adding the well-sampled green back removes most of the false colour for almost no extra cost.
method
Gradient-corrected / edge-directed
Estimate the local edge direction first and interpolate along it, never across it. This is what removes zippers, and it is what every camera actually ships. It is a small, carefully-tuned linear filter with a decision in the middle — precisely the kind of hand-designed operator that a learned network now often replaces.
alternative
No mosaic at all
Three sensors behind a beam splitter, or stacked photodiodes exploiting the wavelength-dependence of silicon's absorption depth. Both measure all three channels at every location and neither needs demosaicing; both cost more, and the stacked design trades colour separation for it.
Why this matters upstream of everything else. Demosaicing is a spatially varying, non-linear operation applied before you ever see the image. It correlates neighbouring pixels, so per-pixel independent noise assumptions are already false at the first step; it manufactures high-frequency structure that a corner detector will happily find; and it is applied before the gamma of §23, so undoing gamma does not undo it. Anything demanding genuinely independent measurements — noise calibration, high-precision photometry, some stereo matching — should start from the raw mosaic, not from the developed image.
The mosaic, the interpolation, and the artefacts it leaves at an edge the zipper and the false colour are computed, not drawn — switch methods and watch them go
  • The smooth colour ramp is reconstructed exactly by all three methods — RMSE 0.00000. Demosaicing only ever fails where there is detail, which is precisely where you were going to look.
  • On the diagonal edge, interpolating the colour differences cuts the invented colour from 0.102 to 0.080 (RMS over the frame), and the gradient method ties with it — at 45° the horizontal and vertical gradients are equal, so the direction test has nothing to choose between and falls back to the plain average.
  • On axis-aligned bars, the gradient method is exact. RMSE drops 0.239 → 0.121 → 0.000 across the three. That is what interpolating along an edge rather than across it buys, when the edge has a direction to find.
  • On the zone plate it is a different story. The pattern runs past the sampling limit, the local gradient estimate becomes meaningless, and the edge-directed method does no better than bilinear (RMSE 0.322 against 0.327) while the constant-hue method is the best of the three. Edge-directed interpolation is only as good as its edge estimate, and above Nyquist there is no edge to estimate — which is Part 3's subject, arriving here a section early.

25 The sensor, its noise, and the pipeline that ends in eight bits

The last stage. Photons arrive, become electrons, become a voltage, become an integer, and then pass through a dozen processing steps before anything is stored. Every one of those steps is a place where the relationship between the stored number and the scene radiance is weakened.

The two sensor technologies

The parameters that decide image quality

time
Shutter speed
Sets how much light arrives, and how much motion blur. Longer is less noisy and more blurred. Blur is not always the enemy — for video, a little is needed to avoid strobing — but for feature matching it is.
space
Sampling pitch
The physical spacing of the cells. A smaller pitch means more pixels for a given chip, and each one collects fewer photons, so resolution is bought directly with noise. "More megapixels" on the same sensor size is always this trade.
space
Fill factor
The fraction of each cell that is actually light-sensitive; the rest is wiring. A higher fill factor gathers more light and aliases less, because averaging over a wider area is a better anti-alias prefilter — which is Part 3's subject. Microlenses exist to raise the effective fill factor toward 1.
amplitude
Analog gain (ISO)
Multiplies the signal before digitisation. It does not improve the signal-to-noise ratio of the photons — it scales signal and shot noise equally — but it does lift both above the downstream read and quantisation noise. That is the whole of what ISO buys.

The claim to be sceptical of. "This sensor is 14-bit." The number of bits the converter emits is an upper bound on the useful ones, not a measurement of them. If the read noise is 3 e⁻ and the well is 10 000 e⁻, there are about log₂(3333) = 11.7 bits of real information no matter how many the converter produces. The honest test is empirical: photograph a uniform patch repeatedly and plot the measured noise against brightness.

The image signal processor, in order

photons→well→analog gain→ADC (12–14 bit)→black level→demosaic→white balance→colour matrix→denoise + sharpen→gamma→8 bit→Y′CbCr + subsample→DCT + quantise + entropy code
  • White balance is, in its simplest form, one multiplier per channel — a diagonal matrix — chosen so that something believed to be white comes out with equal RGB. More careful versions go through XYZ and apply a full 3×3 "colour twist". Both are estimates of the illuminant, and both are wrong when the scene has two illuminants.
  • The colour matrix converts the sensor's own S_R, S_G, S_B responses to a standard space (§22). It is the manufacturer's licence to use whatever filters it likes.
  • Denoise and sharpen are non-linear, spatially varying, and tuned by taste. They are the largest single obstacle to treating a consumer image as a measurement, and the reason "shoot raw" is the standing advice for any photometric work.
  • Compression converts to Y′CbCr, subsamples the chroma (4:2:0 keeps half the samples), transforms 8×8 blocks with the DCT — a good approximation to the eigendecomposition of natural image patches, so it concentrates energy in few coefficients — quantises, and entropy-codes. The quality slider is the quantisation step, and the artefacts are block edges and ringing near strong edges, both of which a feature detector will read as structure.
  • Reported as PSNR: MSE = (1/n)Σ(I − Î)², PSNR = 20 log₁₀(I_max/RMSE). It is convenient and it correlates badly with what anyone can see, which is why perceptual metrics exist.
